## 3. Why university students are the mentor, not the learner
- The adviser is right that an enrolled student has a course in mind; the app would be a glance for them.
  o So they are not the learner, and the funding line excludes them (business-case-v1.md s1).
- The learner end is where the aspiration is formed: a snippet of a University of Newcastle study says interest in "real" jobs appears from Year 4 [7] (snippet).
  o That supports school age; it does not show music practice moves study plans (unverified).
- Student mentors are a documented outreach model: current students mentor Years 7 to 12 and keep contact over time (opened) [4].
  o Other pages (snippet only): ACU ambassadors work with primary and high school; a Queensland report treats student ambassadors as the umbrella term [5][6].
- The mentor job is small and bounded: one screen, one reply, supervisor approves, "cannot assess" allowed (handoff-design-v1.md s4).
  o The supply is a supervised pool of pre-service teachers (UC 9885, Flinders EDUC2338, La Trobe EDU3009), proposed not agreed.
- Not a claim of no need: an enrolled student may still want the app; they are outside this proposed outreach pilot because of the funding line, not because of user need.
- The mentor evidence shows student mentors exist as a model; it does not show that one screen and one reply is a competent music assessment (a judge will ask what a mentor can assess).
- Safeguarding owner is open: the payer row says outreach team, the mentor design relies on a supervisor and checks; nobody has agreed either (judge question: who is accountable for twelve weeks).
- The honest limit: no page I opened says a music-practice tool counts as outreach, or that students want a music mentor.
  o That is a test, not a claim (section 5).
