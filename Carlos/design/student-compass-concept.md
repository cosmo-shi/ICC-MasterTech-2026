# The Student's Compass - concept page (draft 5 Nov 2026)

- One line: a per-assignment AI companion that tells the student what is allowed, points the way without answering, and shows where they are stuck.
- Name logic: a compass gives direction, not the destination. Elden Ring, not waypoints everywhere.
- Answers the question: it changes how the student learns (direction, not answers) and succeeds (stuck map, outcome checks).

## The student's week
| Moment | What the student sees | What it is doing underneath |
|---|---|---|
| Assignment released | A one-screen "how you may use AI here" card: allowed, allowed with declaration, not allowed, for this task | Reads the brief, rubric and unit policy; maps each task part to an AI-use level and a learning outcome |
| Working at 11 pm | A chat that asks back: "what does the case tell you about the patient's risk?" Never a finished paragraph | Guardrail: direction-only mode tied to the card; the student can turn the dial up only where the card allows |
| End of each week | A three-question check mapped to one learning outcome, two minutes | Scores understanding per outcome, not per prompt or token |
| Any time | A personal map: which outcomes are green, which are stuck, suggested next step | Only the student sees it; built from checks and chat, never from device data |
| Before submission | A journey record: what they asked, where they changed their mind, what they learned | Plain-language summary the student reads and owns; can be attached as the Flinders-style AI appendix |

## Five parts
- Card: AI-use rules for this assignment, generated from the brief and rubric, approved by the coordinator.
  o Fixes "I do not know what is allowed", the first thing students fear.
- Guide: Socratic chat that gives one next step, asks back, critiques drafts, never writes the answer.
  o Evidence: hint-only tutor lifted practice 127% and removed exam harm (research bank).
- Checks: short weekly questions mapped to the unit's learning outcomes (outcome-based education).
  o Makes "am I actually understanding" visible before marking week.
- Map: the student's own view of stuck outcomes with a suggested next action.
  o Private by default; no teacher sees an individual map unless the student shares.
- Record: the learning journey, written for the student first, shareable by choice.
  o Turns the prompt appendix sponsors already ask for into something readable.

## What falls out for the university (not the headline)
- Coordinator: anonymous cohort heatmap of stuck outcomes, minimum group size before any cell shows.
  o Answers Rodney's 450-student lecturer: see where the cohort is stuck in week 3, act once for all.
- Marker: opt-in journey view when a student chooses to share, process evidence beside the outcome mark.
- Policy: declared AI use becomes a card and a record instead of a form and a detector.

## Principles
| Principle | Rule |
|---|---|
| Student is the hero | Every demo beat is the student's hands; staff views come after |
| Direction, not answers | The guide may explain, question, critique; it may not produce the deliverable |
| Thinking, not volume | Checks measure understanding; no token counts, prompt counts or time on task |
| Opt-in and scoped | Only activity inside the assignment space exists; the student decides what leaves it |
| No device access | No phone, laptop, screen, keystroke or browser monitoring |
| Data stays home | Runs on university-controlled or local models; nothing to an offshore vendor by default |

## How it differs from Studiosity, Cogniti, Cognito and the rest
- Outcome mapping: no product checked maps a student's work to unit learning outcomes. Ours does, weekly.
- Student-owned stuck map: in Studiosity and Cogniti the institution sees the work; here the student sees first.
- Hosting: every verified incumbent runs in a vendor cloud; ours runs on university or local models.
- Ownership: a framework the university owns, not a per-seat subscription. RMIT already replaced Studiosity with its own tool.
- Honest overlap: Studiosity Validate and Turnitin Clarity already set an AI-use level per assessment.
  o So the card is guidance built from the brief, not our claim. The claim is the outcome map.
- Stronger incumbents: human tutors (Studiosity), scale and retention evidence, free Gemini and Copilot bundles.
- Cognito (school revision): generic syllabus quizzes with AI marking; ours are per assignment, per outcome, feeding a private map.
- Detail and sources: competitor-gap-analysis.md.

## Demo in 60 seconds
- Beat 1: open the brief, the card appears. "Allowed: explain concepts. Not allowed: write the case answer."
- Beat 2: type "write my answer". The guide asks back; the student reasons one step and the map updates.
- Beat 3: the weekly check lights a stuck outcome; the journey record shows what changed since week 1.
