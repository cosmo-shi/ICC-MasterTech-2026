## 1. Bottom line
- Three of our rules a teacher would most likely challenge:
  o 1. The tier order puts pitch (tiers 3 to 5) before timing (tier 6) and note lengths (7). Several teacher sources teach rhythm first and add pitch later.
  o 2. The voice technique cues V4 ("narrow the vowel"), V8 and V9. They turn loudness and wobble numbers into a technique instruction a teacher would want to hear first.
  o 3. The measurement gap. The app sees pitch, timing and loudness only; no posture, hand position or tone rule exists, and by our reading one wrong key in the middle of a phrase fires no rule (K1 is first note only, K2 needs 30% of notes).
- Fastest path to one review by Wednesday:
  o Ask John and Frank first (Sat or Sun): both asked for a real teacher (F19, F85); ask each to name one teacher they know. Carlos sends the 95-word email the same day.
  o In parallel, Carlos or Simone sends it to the team's own contacts and to two local music schools (section 5). Take the first yes; target a 20-minute call Mon 9 or Tue 10 Nov.
  o Run section 4 on one phrase and three takes; fold answers into the cue wording by Tue night; the pitch changes only after a real review (section 7).
- Honest wording:
  o Before a review: "Each take gets one fix, in the style of the one-point lesson used in Suzuki teaching. A music teacher has not yet reviewed our wording."
  o After a review: "A qualified music teacher reviewed the cue list on <date> and we changed <N> cues." Only with a real date and count.
- Do not say "rhythm first" while our tier order is pitch first; the evidence below supports it as a common teaching order, not as a rule.

## 3. Rule-by-rule check
### 3.1 The 20 v1 rules plus X1 and X2 (22 rows; predictions, not teacher statements)
- Caveats from the critique: "likely agree" on V1, V2, V12 and K5 is weaker than it reads (V1 assumes the app can sense tiredness; V12 says "full bar" but some notes are shorter than a bar; K1 does not name the wrong note actually played). Treat all "agree" cells as untested.
- Safety: pain or marked vocal strain should stop practice outright, not compete for a tier; ask the teacher where that line sits (Q9).
| ID | One cue (v1 wording) | A teacher would say | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | Hold the phone closer and play it again. | likely agree | Practical, no teaching claim |
| M2 | Tap along once so the app can set its timing. | unknown | Set-up step; a teacher may not care |
| V1 | Rest now; this line moves to tomorrow. | likely agree | Resting a tired voice is standard advice |
| V2 | Try the lower key the app picked. | likely agree | Comfortable range is basic singing teaching |
| V3 | Hear the guide's first note, then follow the line. | likely agree | Giving the starting pitch is standard |
| V4 | Don't get louder on the high note; narrow the vowel. | likely challenge | "Narrow the vowel" is jargon; a numeric loudness and wobble pattern is a weak basis to diagnose pushing |
| V5 | Aim the high note at the top of the line on screen. | unknown | Plausible; a teacher may prefer a sound target |
| V6 | Hum the first note with the guide, then sing. | likely agree | Matching a hummed note is common |
| V7 | Hear the guide note first, then match it, not above it. | likely agree | Same |
| V8 | Start each note right on the line, like tapping it. | likely challenge | "Like tapping" may cause a hard attack on the voice |
| V9 | Keep the long note as straight as the line on screen. | likely challenge | Breath is the cause; "straight" may stiffen the tone |
| V10 | Breathe in on the beat before you sing. | likely agree | Breath on the beat is standard |
| V11 | Wait for the click, then sing. | likely agree | Simple pulse instruction |
| V12 | Hold each note for its full bar on screen. | likely agree | Note length is basic |
| K1 | Find the first key on screen before you start. | likely agree | Find the start first |
| K2 | Play it at half speed with the click. | likely challenge | Half speed may be too slow; a teacher may cut the phrase instead |
| K3 | Let the click lead; stay with it to the end. | unknown | Rushing is a common beginner problem; a click can also hide the learner's own pulse |
| K4 | Look at the next key while you play this one. | likely agree | Eyes ahead is standard |
| K5 | Count "one-two" aloud and make every note last the same. | likely agree | Counting aloud is common (E1, E2) |
| K6 | Play the last note as gently as the first. | unknown | Teachers may read a rise in loudness as normal |
| X1, X2 | Find the note in the octave shown, then play it again; play every note in the line, slower if you need to. | likely agree | Plain, factual (proposed rules, take-data-contract.md) |

- Tier order (all rules): likely challenge. Rhythm sources (E1 to E3) put timing before pitch; ours puts "can the app hear", tired voice, range, tune and pitch first, timing at tier 6.
  o Our defence to test: a wrong or sour note often is the thing a beginner hears; a teacher may accept it for a note-reading phrase.
- Not covered by any rule: posture, hand position, breath, tone. A teacher will ask what the app does about them; the honest answer is "nothing, it says so".
- The cue wording line a teacher would rewrite first, with a plain rewrite under 12 words:
| Rule | Now | Rewrite (words) |
|---|---|---|
| V4 | Don't get louder on the high note; narrow the vowel. | Sing the high note as softly as the others. (9) |
| K2 | Play it at half speed with the click. | Play slowly, one note on each click. (7) |
| V8 | Start each note right on the line, like tapping it. | Aim straight for each note from the start. (8) |
| V9 | Keep the long note as straight as the line on screen. | Take a calm breath, then hold the long note. (8) |

- Caveat: V4 is the cue behind our single-user evidence (n = 1); the rewrite loses that link, so test both on a take before changing the file.

## 4. The ten-question review sheet (20 minutes, printable)
- One phrase: Ode to Joy bars 1 to 2, E E F G G F E D, C position, 80 bpm (demo-instrument-decision.md; decisions-log.md says the scored voice phrase is the same).
- Three recorded takes, played in this order, no scores shown until question 6:
  o Take A clean. Take B rushed (about 10% to 20% fast; K3 fires). Take C one wrong key on the first note (K1 fires).
- Critique fixes (Codex call 2, kept after check):
  o Add take D: rushed and wrong first key together, and ask which single fix they would give first before the app's cue is shown; this is the only take that tests the tier order.
  o Ask open first, record the answer verbatim, then show the app's cue; allow "give no cue" as an answer. A good answer is not "yes".
  o Keyboard takes cannot test the voice rules (V1 to V12): play one recorded voice take if a safe one exists, else say the voice rules are unreviewed.
  o Ten questions in about ten minutes is tight: ask Q1, Q2, Q4, Q6, Q7 live, the rest as written follow-ups.
- Time plan: minutes 0 to 3 context read aloud; 3 to 8 listen to A to D; 8 to 18 questions; 18 to 20 close and thanks. The reviewer answers aloud; the host writes.

| # | Question | A good answer looks like | If the answer is no, we change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | After hearing take B, what is the first thing you would tell a beginner? | Names timing (or whatever the app named), one thing | Reorder tiers; show the teacher's order to the team |
| 2 | Take B: the app says "Let the click lead; stay with it to the end." Would you say that? | Yes, or a small tweak | Rewrite K3; ask for the wording they use |
| 3 | Take C: the app says "Find the first key on screen before you start." Right fix? | Yes | Rewrite K1 |
| 4 | Is one fix per take the right amount for a beginner? | Yes, with a reason | Keep; if no, ask for the right number and re-open R5 |
| 5 | Which of the app's two cues is unclear to a complete beginner? | One or none | Replace the word they name |
| 6 | Show the scores: any take where the score disagrees with your ear? | None, or a case we can write a rule for | Write the Q35 rule: the teacher wins, log the case |
| 7 | What does the app miss that you would check (posture, hand position, breath, tone)? | A short list | Add an on-screen note "the app cannot see this" |
| 8 | Would you tell a student to try this between lessons, and what would you say first? | Yes, with a caveat | Rewrite the learner promise; add the caveat to the screen |
| 9 | When should a learner stop and see a teacher? | A clear trigger (for example three misses in a row, pain) | Check R10 hand-off trigger against it |
| 10 | Anything we should never say to a beginner? | A list | Add it to the banned-words list |

- Extra, only if time: one wrong key in the middle of the phrase; the app by our reading says nothing (no rule fires). Ask what a teacher would say, then decide whether to add a rule.
- Record: the date, the teacher's role (not name unless they agree), answers, and what changed. That record is what section 7 may cite.

- Never say:
  o "Rhythm first" (our tiers are pitch first), "teacher-approved", "teacher-validated", "pedagogically proven", "research shows one correction at a time works".
  o "Replaces your teacher"; "teaches you to sing"; "musicians love it"; "endorsed by" anyone not named in a real written yes.
  o Anything from the state-of-play s5 do-not-say list or the banned topics in session-rules.md.
  o That the app detects posture, hand position, tone or technique.
