# Nine-student test plan: voice version, two evenings, 20 minutes each

- Date: Fri 6 Nov 2026 (AEDT). Purpose: the learner test that produces the numbers for success criteria C1 to C4 (success-criteria-draft.md).
- Scope: voice-first loop (functional-requirements-v0.md): one phrase, one cue, one retake. Adults only (18+), no names, no audio kept. Chord version is Appendix A.
- Recommendation: 3-3-3 groups where every person ends up seeing the cue, so C3 and C4 always have nine answers.
- Status: overnight draft for the team; verify before the pitch. Risk triage, not legal advice. Items marked `unverified` are not checked.

## 1. What changed from the Friday pack (conclusion first)
- The Friday pack (section 8) tests chord playing, groups A/B/C = app / YouTube / chart. It cannot produce C1 to C4.
  o This plan swaps the task for one 10-second sung phrase with one held note, and one plain-words cue.
- Every participant sings take 1 with no help, then sees the real cue at a different moment by group.
  o A: cue straight after take 1, then take 2.
  o B: no cue after take 1, take 2 is a plain repeat, cue after take 2, then take 3.
  o C: "Try again." after take 1 (generic prompt), take 2, cue after take 2, then take 3.
- Result: nine cue exposures (a take just before the cue, a take just after) for C1, nine cue ratings for C3 and C4, and a small practice-only baseline from B and C.
  o Pitch order (critique kept): lead with take 1 to take 2 for all three groups side by side (A cue, B repeat, C "Try again."). The later B and C cue results are shown separately, labelled "after two attempts".
  o Report all nine outcomes, never only the scorable ones: fewer, same, more, no headroom, unscorable, wrong cue.
  o Ethics catch (verified against the NHMRC text): this design has control groups, a cohort comparison and a prototype, three NHMRC review triggers. Treat it as research for ethics purposes and get a written determination before recruiting (section 3).
- Rejected from the Codex draft: 5 A / 2 B / 2 C. With all nine seeing the cue, A's size no longer drives C3/C4, so equal 3-3-3 gives the cleaner control comparison.

## 3. Consent (read aloud, verbal yes, no signature)
> "This is an informal test of an unfinished music app. You will sing one short phrase a few times and answer some questions. The app listens to your voice to count pitch wobbles. The audio stays on this phone and I delete it in front of you at the end. We keep only anonymous numbers, ratings and short comments. We do not record your name. You can skip any question or stop whenever you like, without giving a reason. It tests the app, not your singing. Are you 18 or over, and are you happy to go ahead?"

- About 100 words (counted by script). Log "yes" on the sheet; if no or not sure, stop and thank them.
  o The script does not tell participants about the three groups (limited disclosure, an NHMRC term). Add one line if the ethics reply requires it: "People may see slightly different screens."
  o Ethics reply may force wording changes (for example saying it is research): edit the script before evening one, not during.
- Do not photograph the screen with a face in it; no video.
- Consistent with privacy-design-v0.md: audio on device, features not audio, no account, no IP-linked logs.
  o Pitch traces (numbers) are kept under the anonymous ID. A contour can still single someone out in principle (privacy-design-v0.md section 1); keep the sheet off shared drives with names and delete after the pitch.

### When university ethics approval would be required instead
- Verdict: ethics approval would be required, or at least a written exemption sought, when any of the following holds; the controlled design here already trips the NHMRC triggers below, so ask first.
  o Adults only; no minors (the 2025 National Statement has a children and young people chapter, with "assent" added; chapter 4.3 per its contents page, the NHMRC Child Safe Policy page says 4.2, so check the PDF before citing a number) [2].
  o Findings are not published or reused beyond our own product decision (a pitch to judges is a grey area: ask the sponsor).
  o Not run as a university-run or course-linked study; UC requires human-research ethics approval for research with human participants (search snippet, privacy-design-v0.md source 15) [4].
  o Risk stays "lower risk". The 2025 National Statement glossary (opened) defines lower risk research as either low risk (no risk of harm, a risk of discomfort, possibly foreseeable burden) or minimal risk (no risk of harm or discomfort, only minor burden or inconvenience) [2]. Singing in front of strangers is probably low risk (discomfort): our reading.
- What NHMRC says (opened, PDFs read via download 2026-11-06):
  o The 2025 National Statement (glossary, and 2.3.8 on QA and evaluation) sends QA and evaluation questions to NHMRC's 2014 guidance "or as updated" [2]; effective-from date 23 Jun 2026 and the removal of automatic full-committee review for increased-risk groups come from search snippets only [2].
  o The 2014 guidance treats an activity as QA when the data are collected to maintain standards, are not linked to individuals, and none of the triggers apply [1]. Listed triggers: privacy or reputation infringement; secondary use of data; gathering more than routine information; testing non-standard (innovative) protocols or equipment; comparison of cohorts; randomisation or control groups; targeted analysis of minority or vulnerable groups [1].
  o Our design hits three: control groups (B, C), cohort comparison (A vs B vs C), and an innovative prototype. It is therefore not QA by NHMRC's test. Consequence: ask the sponsor or the university ethics office for a written determination (exempt, low-risk review, or full review) before inviting anyone. Whether a student team's own product test is in the university's remit is for them to say, not us.
  o Cheapest fallback if approval cannot arrive before Wednesday: run the same script with teammates as a rehearsal, or drop B and C (no control, no comparison) and report A only as a usability check.
- What universities add (snippet): publication, secondary use of data, and testing innovative equipment or protocols bring an activity back into review; exemptions usually need confirmation before the start and cannot be granted afterwards [5].
  o So "it is only an app test" is not self-certifying: ask once, in writing, before evening one.
- Not a substitute for the consent script above: even exempt activities must be ethical [1].

## 4. The voice task (design)
| Item | Fixed value |
|---|---|
| Phrase | One built-in, rights-cleared 10-second phrase with a long held note in the middle (R13); same for all nine |
| Reference | A piano tone for the held note in the participant's comfortable key; one range adjustment allowed, logged |
| Phone | One named low-end phone, one position (a hand-span from the mouth, propped), airplane mode from a cold start every session (feeds C5a) |
| Cue | One plain-words cue of 20 words or fewer, chosen by the app's rules from take 1 (tech-feedback-layer.md section 3), never by the facilitator |
| Eligible cue for C1 | Take 1 cause_class is V9 (wobbly long note) only, so the wobble count matches what the cue targets; other classes (V4 and the rest) are logged and reported separately with their own pre-agreed measure, not counted toward C1 |
| Which take makes the cue | A: take 1. B and C: take 2 (so the cue describes their latest attempt); log processing time from the end of the take that made it. C2 uses all nine |
| Ceiling case | Take 1 has fewer than 3 wobbles: no room to improve; counted as "no headroom", reported, not as a failure |

- Alignment with the canned test set (tech-feedback-layer.md section 9): human case 1 maps to case 10 (V9 wobbly long) for the main path.
  o Log cause_class and cue text per participant; any take 1 where the app returns a class that does not fit what the trace shows is a mismatch to record, not to fix live.
  o Cases 8 (injection) and 15 (out of scope) stay on the canned test; the human goal sentence is typed by the facilitator only as a log note and is not sent as `learner_words` unless the team decides otherwise (Open questions).

## 5. The 20-minute script
- A slot is 20 minutes. Script runs 16 minutes (A) or 18 minutes (B, C); the rest is reset and handover.
- The facilitator reads only the quoted lines. A second person logs and times; neither speaks otherwise.
- Group is assigned from a pre-shuffled list before evening one (section 9).

| Min | All groups: action and verbatim line |
|---|---|
| 0-1 | Welcome: "Thanks for coming. I will read a short note." Read consent. If no, stop |
| 1-3 | Cold start in airplane mode; check mic permission and room. "We are checking the phone can hear you." Play the reference tone: "Can you sing this note comfortably?" One adjustment only; log it |
| 3-4 | "In your own words, what would you like this phrase to sound like?" Log the sentence. Then: "This tests the app, not your singing." |
| 4-5 | "You will see a line on screen and a guide note. Please try the phrase once to get used to it. This one does not count." (unrecorded warm-up) |
| 5-6 | "When the screen says go, sing the phrase." Take 1 recorded. Log take-end time |

| Min (A) | Min (B and C) | Action and verbatim line |
|---|---|---|
| 6-7 | 6-7 | A: cue appears; log seconds from take end to cue. Facilitator: "Take your time with the screen." B: screen says "Ready for take 2"; nothing else. C: screen says "Try again." Facilitator says nothing |
| 7-8 | 7-8 | "Please sing the phrase again when you are ready." Take 2 recorded |
| - | 8-9 | B and C only: cue appears; log seconds from take end to cue. "Take your time with the screen." |
| - | 9-10 | B and C only: "Please sing the phrase again when you are ready." Take 3 recorded |
| 8-9 | 10-11 | Open question first: "What, if anything, stood out to you?" Log words, no prompting |
| 9-10 | 11-12 | "How much do you agree: after that screen, I know what to do next? One is not at all, five is completely." |
| 10-11 | 12-13 | Trust: "How much do you trust that suggestion for your next try? One is not at all, five is completely." |
| 11-12 | 13-14 | Usefulness: "How much do you agree: the suggestion was understandable and worth trying? One is not at all, five is completely." |
| 12-13 | 14-15 | Open first: "What, if anything, felt like extra work?" Log verbatim. Then: "Overall, did it feel like help, neither, or homework?" |
| 13-14 | 15-16 | "Would you like to do another phrase now? Yes, no, or not sure?" Then: "Why?" (If yes, run one unscored phrase.) |
| 14-15 | 16-17 | "Was anything confusing or uncomfortable?" Log briefly |
| 15-16 | 17-18 | Check sheet for blanks, do not change answers. "Thanks. We were checking whether one short suggestion helps. Anything else you want to say?" Delete both or all three audio files in front of them, say "Deleted", reset the app |

### Must-not-say list
- No praise or blame: not "good", "nice", "better", "that was off", "you improved".
- No technique words: not "breath", "support", "pitch", "flat", "sharp", "wobble", "steady", "vowel" unless the screen shows them.
- No explaining or demonstrating the cue; no repeating it aloud; no humming the note again.
- No hint of what the groups are, what the "right" answer is, or that the app is expected to help.
- Do not say "AI". If asked, say "the app suggests one thing to try".
- No rating-pushing: not "just a quick four?"; never fill silences with reassurance.
- If asked for help: "I can't help during the test; do whatever feels right."
- Never mention music or songs the participant might know; use only the built-in phrase.

- Courtesy bias (critique kept): ratings go on a printed card the participant circles and hands back face down; the facilitator reads out only the question wording.
  o "Another phrase": ask "Would you like to do another phrase now?" and, if yes, run one unscored; log "offered" and "took it". The "if we had time" hypothetical is dropped.
- Wording basis: Henderson et al. 2025 surveyed helpfulness and trustworthiness of GenAI feedback among about 7,000 students (83.9% helpful, 60.1% trusted, via research-bank.md) [3].
  o Their exact item text was not re-read here; ours is an adaptation, so call it "Henderson-style", never "the Henderson scale". `unverified` item wording.
- The success-criteria bar for C3 is 6 of 9; Henderson's 60% is context only, and a confident wrong cue can score well (expert check of the nine causes is separate: success-criteria-draft.md).
- Report counts of 9, never a percentage ("7 of 9").
- B and C rate the cue after take 3, so they have had more attempts than A: note it in the results.

## 7. Wobble count: one definition, same number for everyone
- Two scorers get the same number when they apply these steps to the saved pitch trace (numbers only, no audio), never to ears.
  o Segment: the held note only, from 250 ms after its detected start to 250 ms before its end.
  o Smooth: take the median over a 100 ms window of the pitch track (cents).
  o Reference line: the median pitch of that segment (not the target note), so a steady flat or sharp note is not a wobble.
  o Excursion: smoothed pitch more than 50 cents from the reference line for at least 100 ms in a row. It ends when pitch is back within 50 cents for at least 100 ms.
  o Count: one wobble per excursion. A long excursion counts once; vibrato within +-50 cents counts zero.
  o Unscorable: fewer than 70% of segment frames have clear voiced pitch (clarity 0.9 or more, tech-feedback-layer.md section 3 and tech-pitch-detection.md); marked unscorable, never zero.
  o Octave jumps (about +-1200 cents) lasting under 100 ms are tracker artefacts: not counted, noted.
- Worked example: reference line 0; smoothed track within 50 cents until 1.0 s, 70 cents high for 0.3 s, back in range, then 60 cents low for 0.15 s, then in range = 2 wobbles.
- The app may also log "frames where pitch flips over 50 cents frame to frame" (tech-feedback-layer.md section 2); that is the number behind "24 to 7".
  o Decision for the team: C1 headline = the excursion count above (humans can verify it); the frame count is a second number, labelled differently. Do not mix them in one sentence.
- Two scorers count each trace separately, then compare; keep both independent counts and the disagreement rate in the sheet, then reconcile against the rule. Freeze the rule before evening one; a rule edit after evening one is reported as a change.
- Limits: the median reference hides a steadily flat or sharp note (report mean cents off target as an optional second number); the tracker may fail on a beginner's voice, and that is a product finding, not merely missing data.
- Show, for the pitch, one trace with the counted excursions marked.
- Consequence: audio is deleted, so the team cannot show participant audio next to the number (success-criteria-draft.md C1 asks for audio next to the number). For the demo use a teammate's recording with consent.

## 9. Group assignment and what each comparison can show
| Group | Sequence | n | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | take 1, cue, take 2 | 3 | Cue straight after take 1 |
| B | take 1, nothing, take 2, cue, take 3 | 3 | Practice-only baseline, then cue |
| C | take 1, "Try again.", take 2, cue, take 3 | 3 | Generic prompt baseline, then cue |

- Assign from a list shuffled by dice or a random generator before evening one; do not let the facilitator pick by impression. Each evening gets at least one of each group (for example 2 A, 1 B, 1 C on the first, the rest on the second; if a no-show breaks this, take the next standby for the missing group).
- Alternative 5-2-2 (A 5, B 2, C 2): a tighter estimate for the immediate cue, weaker baselines. Use only if the team cares more about an A-only number than about control evidence.
| Comparison | Can show | Cannot show |
|---|---|---|
| All 9: before-cue take vs after-cue take (C1 headline) | How many people had fewer counted wobbles after the cue | That the cue caused it (practice, warm-up, fatigue also move counts); effect size in a population |
| A take 1 to 2 vs B take 1 to 2 (cue vs plain repeat) | A rough check of whether a repeat alone moves the count | Any significance; three people each |
| A take 1 to 2 vs C take 1 to 2 (cue vs generic "Try again.") | Whether a generic nudge looks as good | Whether the specific content matters beyond a nudge |
| B and C take 2 to 3 | The cue after two attempts: a harder test | Clean comparison with A (practice stacked) |
| Trust, usefulness, help or homework | Whether novices say yes, in a convenience sample | Retention; learning; teacher-grade correctness; anything beyond "a first-hour reaction" |
- State the honesty line from the Friday pack: one hour measures novelty, not learning; point to the 12-week pilot.
- Report "x of y" with the denominator and missing data; no percentages and no p-values.

## 10. The honest pitch sentence for each outcome
| Outcome | Rule (set before the test) | Sentence |
|---|---|---|
| Improved | At least 5 of 9 cue exposures had fewer wobbles (denominator is all nine, with no-headroom, unscorable and wrong-cue cases shown), and fewer of the six B and C take 1 to 2 pairs fell than of A's three; show each person's counts, no more claimed than "the three A results were X; the three B results were Y" | "In a nine-person test, x of y beginners had fewer counted wobbles after one plain-words cue; our small control groups improved less, so it is a signal, not proof." |
| Improved, controls improved too | At least 5 of 9 improved, but B or C also improved at a similar rate | "x of y improved on the retake, but people who got no cue improved too, so a retake alone may explain much of it; the pilot is the real test." |
| Mixed | 3 or 4 of 9 improved, or the cue did not match the trace for several | "Some improved (x of y) and some did not; the cue helps sometimes and we know which cases to fix." |
| No change | Fewer than 3 of 9 improved | "The cue did not reduce counted wobbles in this test (x of y); we are revising it before claiming an effect." |
- The threshold numbers are the team's to move before evening one; do not change them after seeing results.
- Trust, usefulness and homework are reported alongside, one sentence each, even if C1 is no change.
- Never claim learning, teacher-level quality, or a percentage.

## 11. Pitfalls and mitigations
| Pitfall | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Novelty and retake practice | B and C baselines; say "first-hour reaction" |
| Facilitator leaks help | Verbatim script, must-not-say list, second person logs slips |
| Participant cannot match the note | One range adjustment; otherwise unscorable, kept for usability only |
| Phone mic noise, second device | Quiet room, fixed position, airplane mode; count unusable as missing |
| No headroom (clean take 1) | Flag and report separately; no coaching, no extra takes |
| Cue about the wrong thing | Log cause_class and match to trace; report mismatches as a finding |
| Cue delay beyond 20 s | Count as a miss, log load vs processing time |
| One participant hates it | Keep the exact words; count their ratings |
| Recording leaks | Delete audio in front of the participant; trace numbers only; no cloud audio |
| Hawthorne effect (trying harder when watched) | Same facilitator behaviour in all groups; second person out of eyeline |
| Ethics approval question | Written check with the sponsor or ethics office before evening one |
