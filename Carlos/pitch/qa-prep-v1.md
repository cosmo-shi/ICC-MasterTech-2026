## 1. Customer value
### Q1. Who exactly is the learner?
- A young person without access: regional, rural, remote or low income; adult beginners welcome (state-of-play s2).
- Age group is a Sunday decision; the demo uses young adult beginner language (accessibility-design-v0.md s1, s8).
- Reached through university outreach (school students), so it fits "students learn and succeed"; hobby-learner fit is open with Jeff (questions-for-sponsor 21).
- Dropped the adult-player statistic as demand evidence: it does not show demand from our learners (judge-cross review).

### Q2. Why would a university pay for this?
- Spoken version (judge pass, under 35 words): "We think a university outreach team could buy it. None has agreed. To start, a budget owner must confirm that this music activity qualifies for outreach funding."
- One proposed buyer: a sponsor university's outreach team, paying from its Outreach Funding allocation; no buyer has agreed yet (business-case-v1.md s9).
- The money exists: 2026 Outreach Funding grants UC $384,182, Flinders $902,208, La Trobe $1,419,938 (business-case-v0.md s1 [S7]; UC confirmed in verification-overdrive.md s1 fix 2).
- Catch, said plainly: the money must build interest in higher education, not recruit or teach music for its own sake; so the pilot measures interest in further study at week 1 and week 12, apart from enjoying music (business-case-v0.md s1; self-assessment-v0.md s5 row 2).
- First condition to proceed: a named outreach budget owner confirms in writing that a music-led activity is eligible and funded (business-case-v1.md s9 row 1).
### Q3. Why now?
- Spoken version (judge pass, under 35 words): "The funding programme changed this year, so outreach teams may be reviewing activities. We have not shown that this creates demand for our app."
- Students already ask AI for feedback: 49.7% of 6,960 surveyed university students (Henderson 2025; verification-overdrive.md s2, confirmed).
- Music in surveyed primary teaching degrees fell from 17 hours (2009) to about 8 (Fading Notes 2023; confirmed, verification-overdrive.md s2).
- HEPPP ended 1 Jan 2026 and the Outreach Funding Program replaced it, so outreach teams are setting up new activities and evaluations now (business-case-v0.md s1).
- Dropped "Music Australia's May 2026 report": a search found no such report (verification-overdrive.md s1 fix 14).

### Q4. Why not just YouTube, or Yousician? (objection replies from competitor-feedback-mechanics-v1.md s5a)
- Spoken version (judge pass, under 35 words): "Yousician already scores playing. Our demo adds one plain-language fix and checks the retake. We have not proved that learners prefer that loop."
- "Yousician already listens, scores and gives exercises." Reply: "Yes: its pages state instant accuracy and timing feedback, goals and lesson plans. Our step is one explained fix, then a retake that shows the change; the demo shows it on one phrase."
- "June already takes my goal and picks the next step." Reply: "Yes; its listing says tell June your goal and a tailored next step every session. The same listing is silent on cause and on before-and-after; we have not tested the app, so we say not stated, not that it does not."
- "Recordings, progress and teachers already exist." Reply: "Yes: Singing Carrots recaps what changed since earlier sessions, Simply Sing saves recordings, Lessonface and EarMaster serve teachers. Our step joins the retake to a referral decision and hands a teacher the recording and the fix list; a teacher link exists elsewhere, the loop that triggers it we did not find."
- Positioning line: "A practice coach for beginners that turns your own goal into small steps, explains one cause and one fix per take, checks the retake, and passes you to a human teacher when it reaches its limit." (competitor-feedback-mechanics-v1.md s1)
- Closest singing rival: Singing Carrots AI coach (goals, coach memory, per-exercise numbers, session recap); its pages read do not state cause-and-fix, offline or referral (same, s1).
## 2. Evidence
### Q5. What have you actually tested?
- Carlos's own practice record: two bursts (26 Aug, 4 Sep 2026), reconstructed from logs; n = 1 (research-bank.md first-hand evidence).
- The demo: take 1 replayed through the real scoring code, the retake live; the number on screen is what the retake scored (demo-instrument-decision.md s4).
- Concept tested on advisors over three hub days, not on learners (state-of-play s4).
- No learner test yet; the nine-person test needs an ethics determination first (Q22).

### Q6. Isn't one user just an anecdote?
- Yes: n = 1, reconstructed measurements, causation untested; a design source, not proof (judge-cross review wording, kept).
- We show the rows against us: eleven mixes never chosen, no teacher referral, he wanted a result, not lessons (research-bank.md rows 3 to 5).
- What would convince: a learner test with a no-cue comparison plus a teacher checking the fixes (nine-student-test-plan.md s1; business-case-v0.md s3 quality check).

### Q7. Students trust teachers more than AI. Isn't that fatal?
- Spoken version (judge pass, under 35 words): "Students in that survey trusted teacher feedback more. That supports keeping a human hand-off; it does not prove they will trust our cues."
- It is our design brief: of the 3,328 who had used GenAI feedback, 83.9% found it helpful (82.2% for teachers) but 60.1% trusted it, against 90.5% for teachers (Henderson 2025; verification-overdrive.md s1 fix 4).
- So one small fix the learner can check on the retake, and a human at the ceiling.
- Caveat: volunteers, about 5% of those invited, four universities, written feedback, perceptions only (verification-overdrive.md s2).

### Q8. How solid is the "eight hours" figure?
- Fading Notes 2023: average 7.98 compulsory hours in a generalist primary degree, range 1 to 19, down from 17 in 2009 (confirmed, verification-overdrive.md s2).
- 36 of 49 invited institutions replied; hours are respondent estimates; ACT average 6.5; 58% of students do not observe or take part in a music lesson on practicum (same).
- Not every teacher's preparation; an average across surveyed degrees (decisions-log.md overnight row).

## 3. Feasibility
### Q9. Does it work offline on a phone, and is it accurate?
- Spoken version (judge pass, under 35 words): "The stage keyboard gives exact MIDI note times. Voice tracking on an ordinary phone, especially in a real room, has not been validated."
- On stage: laptop Chrome on localhost with the MIDI keyboard; MIDI note events are exact, so the timing number is measured, not guessed (build-plan-v0.md s7 scoring contract).
- Phone: a target, not a result; first install needs a connection because a service worker does not control the page until a reload (web.dev, verification-overdrive.md s1 fix 7).
- Voice on a phone: the tracker is tested on synthetic tones only; real-room accuracy is the top technical risk (demo-instrument-decision.md s2; accessibility-design-v0.md s1).
- Proof we owe: C5, three cold-start offline sessions on one named low-end phone (success-criteria-draft.md C5).

### Q10. What does the AI cost per learner?
- Spoken version (judge pass, under 35 words): "Our model-call estimate is small, but it depends on routing. The pilot budget is dominated by staff and teacher time; these are estimates, not spending."
- Known estimate: about US$0.04 per 20-minute session with routing (tech-feedback-layer.md s1, s6); A$0.014 to A$0.095 per session across routing choices (business-case-v1.md).
- For 50 learners over 12 weeks the model calls are about A$112, hosting A$87 (business-case-v1.md s3c lines 2 and 3).
- The cost is people, not compute: staff and teacher time is about 99% of the core pilot cost (business-case-v1.md s3c).
- On stage no model call runs; the cue comes from rules on the device (build-plan-v0.md s4).
### Q11. You would record minors. What about privacy?
- Spoken version (judge pass, under 35 words): "Our design keeps audio on the device unless the learner shares a clip. The cloud would receive their typed goal and words. Child consent and overseas processing need review."
- On stage say "device", not "phone", because the rig is a laptop (coordinator rule): "Audio stays on the device; the cloud coach sees numbers and your typed goal; a teacher hears a clip only if you share."
- One honest sentence (privacy-design-v1.md s6 wording): "Audio stays on the device; the cloud coach sees numbers and your typed goal; a teacher hears a clip only if you share." (privacy-design-v1.md s6)
- Sharing a recording is a separate opt-in per recording, an expiring, revocable link; an expiring link cannot recall a download, so the teacher signs in and access is logged (privacy-design-v0.md s1).
- Under 15, a guardian approves each share and sees the log; the Children's Online Privacy Code is still a draft (child consent alone only from 15 in the exposure draft) (privacy-design-v1.md s6).
- "Where does the cloud coach run?" "Overseas: the provider offers no Australian region today; a pilot would decide that in the privacy impact assessment." (privacy-design-v1.md s6)
- On stage nothing leaves the laptop and no real learner appears; ids like "Learner 12", never first names.
- Never: "privacy compliant", "only anonymous numbers leave the phone", "your data stays in Australia"; say "designed to".
### Q12. What if there is no internet, or no instrument?
- 30.5% of very remote users report connection problems always or often, against 15.9% nationally (ADII 2025, confirmed; say "users").
- The practice loop is designed to run offline, not yet validated (build-plan-v0.md s1).
- No instrument: on-screen keys, the same fallback the stage uses (demo-instrument-decision.md s5).
- Instrument loans and rental from about $12 a month: unverified (verification-overdrive.md s2).

## 5. The hard ones
### Q16. Aren't you just another tutor app? Prior art?
- Spoken version (judge pass, under 35 words): "We reviewed product pages for 27 products and did not find this complete loop documented. A page scan cannot show what a product does behind its pages."
- Listening and goals are common ground (most of 27 products; June, Singing Carrots, Yousician, Vocal Image state goals or plans) (competitor-feedback-mechanics-v1.md s1).
- We found no page-documented example of cause in plain words plus one fix plus before-and-after on the retake among 27 products; page silence is not proof of absence (same).
- Never "first", "nobody" or "own goal" as the difference. If asked what it does with music: "It listens and coaches." (say nothing about generating music, either way)
### Q17. Won't AI replace music teachers?
- Spoken version (judge pass, under 35 words): "No. Our proposal includes a teacher hand-off and paid teacher time. We have not shown that it saves teachers time."
- No: the ceiling hands the learner to a human with the fix list (R10).
- Users trust teachers more (90.5% vs 60.1%), so the human stays central (Henderson 2025, users only).
- The pilot pays for teacher hours; it makes each hour go further (business-case-v0.md s6).

### Q18. Isn't hiding fundamentals inside the learner's goal a trick?
- Nothing is hidden: two visible lines, "your goal" and "your skill" (state-of-play s2).
- Our own record cuts both ways: he wanted a result, not lessons (research-bank.md row 5).
- Related research motivates it (autonomy, progress monitoring) but does not validate this design; criterion C4 asks learners directly (success-criteria-draft.md C4).

### Q19. Why universities and not schools?
- Universities already run free outreach into small rural schools and must spend Outreach Funding on priority cohorts and report it (verification s3; business-case-v0.md s2).
- Schools stay the setting; who buys inside a school is not established.

### Q20. What does Cisco have to do with it?

- Never: "built on Cisco", "integrated with Webex", "Cisco-backed pilot", "Meraki keeps it offline" (cisco-fit-note.md s5).

## 6. Panel questions the cross-check found missing or weak
### Q21. Which parts of that demo were live?
- Take 1 was a replay of a take captured Saturday, run through the real scoring code; the retake was live on the MIDI keyboard (demo-instrument-decision.md s4).
- The hand-off card and Webex message are a scripted mock, labelled proposed (cisco-fit-note.md s4).
- If the recording ran, say so first: "the same run, recorded yesterday".

### Q22. Will you test with students before Wednesday? (ethics)
- Maybe not: the nine-person design has control groups, a cohort comparison and a prototype, three NHMRC review triggers, so it counts as research (nine-student-test-plan.md s1).
- We need a written determination from the sponsor or a university ethics office before recruiting anyone (same, s3).
- Fallback: teammates as a rehearsal, or a cue-only usability check with no comparison; we would not quote its numbers as evidence (nine-student-test-plan.md s3).

### Q23. Where is the AI? Could a pitch detector and rules do it?
- Spoken version (judge pass, under 35 words): "Today, measured notes feed rules that choose the cue. A proposed cloud model would turn the typed goal into steps and phrase the cue around it. We have not shown it improves the result."
- Two layers (tech-feedback-layer.md s1): Layer 1 = rules on the device map the measured features to one cause, cue and proof, offline; Layer 2 = a cloud language model reads those features plus the learner's typed words and goal and returns one schema-locked answer, used when online.
- Routing: one clear rule and no learner words = rules only, no model call; learner words, two close rules or a hand-off candidate = the larger model; rephrasing into the learner's goal = the smaller model (same).
- On stage: cues come from rules; the model layer is off or shows cached answers, labelled "example output" if shown (build-plan-v0.md s4; tech-feedback-layer.md s1; pitch-v1.md section 0 row 11).
- One sentence: "Rules catch what was measured; the model turns it into your words and your plan." (self-assessment-v0.md s5)
- Rules against model wording on the same task: not tested yet.
### Q24. What fails on an ordinary phone in a noisy home?
- Not tested: devices, noise, latency and abstention are unmeasured (judge-cross review).
- Designed guard: an empty or unclear take gives "we heard nothing", never a music cue (build-plan-v0.md s7).
- That is why the stage uses MIDI: no microphone in an expo hall (demo-instrument-decision.md s1).

### Q25. Did the learner learn, or just perform better right after a cue?
- Immediate performance only; the retake shows the fix on that phrase (success-criteria-draft.md honesty line: one hour measures novelty, not learning).
- The pilot measures later: practice in weeks 8 to 12 and whether the fix holds (business-case-v0.md s3).

### Q26. How do you know the score measures improvement, not familiarity?
- On keys, the timing number comes from exact MIDI note times (build-plan-v0.md s7).
- A second try can improve without any cue; the nine-person design has repeat-without-cue groups for that reason (nine-student-test-plan.md s1).
- Independent teacher ratings: a teacher scores a sample of fixes before the pilot (business-case-v0.md s3).

### Q27. How do you choose the next fundamental without teaching the wrong thing?
- A fixed list (right notes, steady pulse, note length, even touch, range, phrase shape) in a set order, written by hand (build-plan-v0.md s7).
- Cause text names only what was measured, never a guess about fingers or technique (same).
- Teacher review of the goal templates: not yet arranged.

### Q28. Who owns academic quality and the referrals?
- To confirm with the sponsor; our proposal is a pre-launch teacher check of the fixes and the university supplying supervised mentors (business-case-v0.md s3).

### Q29. Which young people can you recruit next month, and through whom?
- None confirmed; the university supplies school contacts and the consent path in the pilot model (business-case-v0.md s3).

### Q30. Who handles consent, shared phones and a child with no quiet room?
- Consent: guardian sets up for a child, approves each share (privacy-design-v0.md s3).
- Shared phones, staff time, troubleshooting: not yet designed; to confirm with the sponsor's outreach staff.

### Q31. What if the family still cannot afford a teacher?
- In the pilot the learner pays nothing; the outreach budget pays the hand-off hours (business-case-v0.md s1, s3).
- After the pilot: not yet costed. Cheaper route under test: supervised student mentors.

### Q32. What evidence would fund a second cohort?
- Spoken version (judge pass, under 35 words): "We would look for continued practice, fixes that hold, and useful hand-offs. The sponsor and team still need to agree numerical thresholds before a pilot."
- Go if most learners still practise in weeks 8 to 12, most retakes show a measured fix, and hand-offs happen; no-go if use collapses after week 4 or the fix does not hold (business-case-v0.md s3).
- Thresholds set with the sponsor before the term; none invented here.

### Q33. What does one active learner cost, all in?
- Spoken version (judge pass, under 35 words): "Our estimate is A$342 per enrolled learner at 50 learners. We cannot give a cost per active learner until we know how many stay active."
- Core about A$342 per learner for the 50-learner pilot, mostly staff time (business-case-v1.md s3c).
- Software is about A$4 per learner for 12 weeks (A$199 for 50, lines 2 and 3; same).
### Q34. Goal met, skill line flat: celebrate or keep practising?
- Not decided; the engine banks a fundamental only when its pass rule is met (build-plan-v0.md s7). Team to set the copy (Simone owns copy).

### Q35. A teacher says the take sounds better, your score says worse. Who wins?
- The teacher. No adjudication rule is written yet; the pre-launch teacher check is where we would find these cases (business-case-v0.md s3).

### Q36. Would your best cue work as well on a printed card?
- Possibly, for one cue; the app's job is choosing which cue from what was measured, then checking the retake. No cue-only comparison exists yet.
