# Trend check: AI-assisted music learning

- Purpose: devil's-advocate check on the music-learning idea before the team picks a direction on Fri 6 Nov.
- Date: 5 Nov 2026.
- Method: web search plus page reads; every claim cites a numbered source in section 10.
  o "Unverified" means the fact came from a search snippet or a secondary page, not the vendor's own page.
  o Prices are as shown on the US App Store or vendor site; "$" without a code is presumed USD.

## 1. The idea as proposed on 5 Nov

- Lower the barrier to music learning for students without funds, a teacher, school music, or nearby access.
- A digitised instrument on the phone, from piano and guitar to culturally specific ones such as a Chinese flute.
- AI personalises lessons to the student's level; it never generates music for the student.
- Guitar coaching through the phone camera: track fingers, recognise chords and hand position, then coach.
- Bonus: cultural preservation of rare instruments; inclusion for students with disability or in remote areas.

- Pattern 1: the guitar and piano basics market is mature, large and cheap (1, 8, 15).
  o Yousician claims 20M+ monthly users, flowkey 15M learners, Justin Guitar 4.5M students.

  o Only ROLI coaches posture and finger placement from cameras; it needs ROLI keys, an iPad and Airwave (40).

  o Shipping or announced: Yousician "June", ROLI AI Music Coach, Chordie AI, tau AI, Guru (3, 40, 52, 53, 54).

- Pattern 5: culturally specific instruments are thin: tuners, notation, video, a few pitch-feedback apps (32 to 36).
  o No erhu, guzheng, sitar or Indigenous instrument app found that coaches finger position or posture.

## 3. Camera and sensing feasibility

| Approach | What it detects | Shipped example | Phone demo in one week | Known limits | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Microphone pitch and onset | Notes, timing, tuning | Yousician, Trala, Riyaz, erhu and dizi apps | Yes, single notes and timing | Chords harder; vendor claims 99% for two notes, 90% for three | 1, 24, 29, 33 |
| MIDI from a digital instrument | Exact notes, timing, velocity | flowkey, Piano Marvel, ROLI | Yes, if a keyboard is on hand | Needs an instrument, which defeats the no-instrument equity aim | 9, 23 |
| Phone camera with MediaPipe Hands | 21 3D landmarks per hand; 12 to 17 ms pipeline on a Pixel 6 | No guitar product shipped | Yes for 3 to 5 static chord shapes, staged lighting | Model trained on about 30K images, not instrument play | 58 |
| Hand plus fretboard pose model | Which finger presses which string and fret | Research only (IEEE Access 2026) | No | Generic hand trackers cannot place fingertips on frets; no dedicated dataset until this work | 62 |
| Overhead infrared cameras | 27 joints per hand at 90 fps; posture, fingering | ROLI Airwave and AI Music Coach (beta) | No, dedicated hardware | iPad only; ROLI keys required | 39, 40 |
| Headset hand tracking | Finger placement on keys | Simply Piano on Vision Pro, PianoVision | No | Headset cost; not a phone | 6, 56 |
| Audio plus vision fusion | Fingertip on string, inferred from pitch | Research (ACM TOG 2024; WACV 2025) | No | Generic pose estimators struggle with instrument occlusion | 60, 61 |
| Wearable sensors on the bow | Bow angle and tilt | Research (Sensors 2024) | No | Extra hardware; feedback raised cognitive load | 63 |

- Justin's guitar camera idea is plausible for a demo but unsolved as a product.
  o MediaPipe runs in a browser and on phones; a fixed-angle demo of three open chords is achievable (58).
  o Real accuracy needs fretboard pose as well as hand pose; research only reached this in 2026 (62).
  o Papers name occlusion, partial views and hand-object contact as the core failure (60, 61).
- Justin's piano point is half right: a phone facing the player cannot see the keys, but overhead cameras can.
  o ROLI uses an infrared camera above the keys (39); PianoMotion10M holds 116 hours of bird's-eye video (59).
- Camera posture coaching for violin and guitar exists only as prototypes or waitlists.
  o Maryland's violin posture project was a 2021 research article (57); Guru's guitar coach is a waitlist (52).
- The one controlled feedback trial found used bow sensors, not a camera, with 24 adults (63).
  o Bow-angle error fell from 9.47 to 4.32 degrees, but only 16.67% found playing with feedback not difficult.
- Demo honesty: the mentor said a proof of concept can be faked; judges may still ask "does it work?".
  o A staged three-chord demo is fair if labelled; claiming posture coaching would overreach.

## 4. Evidence on app-based instrument learning

| Study or dataset | Finding | Strength | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fender, executive estimate (2019) | About 90% of new guitar players quit within a year; about 45% of guitars go to first-time players | Weak: interview remark, no method | 13 |
| Fender Play vendor case study | "Nearly 95% retention rate" and 200,000+ paying customers within three years | Weak: metric undefined, vendor marketing | 14 |
| Effectiveness of special apps for online piano lessons, Interactive Learning Environments (2022) | 350 students; first year: Skoove 78.9, Simply Piano 78.3, no-app control 72.4; fifth year: 81.5, 81.4 vs 74.7 | Moderate to weak: one study, abstract only, apps vs no app, not vs a teacher | 64 |
| Adults learning an instrument with an app, Lancaster PhD thesis (2024) | Apps "democratised music education"; learners often frustrated by instructional and technical limits | Weak to moderate: autoethnography plus interviews | 66 |
| Yousician case study, International Journal of Creative Multimedia (2021) | Four guitarists, one week; results not visible on the page | Very weak | 67 |
| Serious games for learning music, systematic review, J. Computer Assisted Learning (2025) | 32 studies; games generally effective for learning and engagement; most games no longer available | Moderate: review of mixed-quality studies | 65 |
| Intelligent tutoring systems in US K-12, meta-analysis (arXiv, Nov 2025) | 18 studies, 77 effect sizes; overall g = 0.271; worked examples a top moderator | Moderate: preprint, school maths and literacy, not music | 68 |
| AI-driven tutoring systems in K-12, systematic review, npj Science of Learning (2025) | 28 studies, 4,597 students; only 1 of 4 comparisons with non-intelligent systems favoured the AI system | Moderate; personalisation not isolated, novelty effects noted | 69 |
| Real-time visual feedback on violin bowing, Sensors (2024) | 24 adult beginners, 12 per group; feedback group errors fell, 9.47 to 4.32 degrees; task felt hard | Weak to moderate: one session, allocation not described | 63 |
| LLM teachable agent for music theory, CHI 2025 workshop (arXiv) | 28 Chinese university students; teaching group scored significantly higher with lower cognitive load | Weak: small, preprint | 70 |
| Duolingo Q2 2026 earnings call | Math and Music have "single-digit millions" of daily users, "much smaller than chess" | Adoption signal only | 21 |

- No independent trial shows app learners progress as well as taught learners.
  o The best controlled study found apps beat no app by about 8% (64); no vendor publishes a trial.
- Retention is the industry's admitted problem, which is why streaks and levels dominate (13, 14).
  o Vendors quote retention or improvement without definitions (14, 16, 17, 25).
- Gamification evidence in music is about engagement more than durable skill (65).
- "AI personalisation" has no demonstrated effect beyond adaptive difficulty in the reviews found.
  o AI tutors beat non-intelligent systems in 1 of 4 head-to-head comparisons (69).
  o K-12 tutoring effects are small (g = 0.271); worked examples matter more than adaptivity (68).
- More feedback is not automatically better: beginners found playing plus live feedback hard (63).
- One link to the team's other work: learning by teaching showed gains in university music theory (70).

- Stanley's distinction holds: musicians accept AI for practice (68%) far more than for generation (18%) (71).
  o Caveat: both musician surveys are self-selected users of music-tech platforms (71, 72).
- The mentor's warning lands on output and culture, not on basic lessons.
  o Listener hostility targets generated tracks (73); creator fear targets income (74).
  o "Cultural preservation" of instruments touches First Nations cultural rights directly (75).
- Adoption signal: even Duolingo's free music course stays "much smaller than chess" (21).
- Gap: no survey found of university students on AI for learning an instrument.

- Equity: the best-documented Australian barrier is teacher capability and time, not app cost (76, 77).
  o Apps are cheap or free (5, 81, 82); the instrument and skilled feedback are the scarce parts.
  o A phone-only instrument removes instrument cost but cannot build real-instrument technique (analysis).
- Disability and regional access: the programmes found could not be opened, so they are not used.
  o Melbourne's Adaptive Music Bridging Program and a remote-tuition case study: pages blocked (403).
  o "One-third of secondary students lack school music": advocacy figure, submission blocked, unverified.
- University relevance: a hobby app for children does not answer "students learn and succeed" at university.
  o Two credible anchors exist: beginner music units (79) and pre-service teachers' music training (76, 77, 84).
  o Pre-service primary teachers are expected to meet music curriculum needs after about 8 hours of it (77).
  o A creative-arts degree anchor is weaker: those students already have teachers and ensembles (analysis).

## 7. The twelve-test filter applied to the idea as proposed

| # | Test | Score | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | Student visibly learns differently in the demo | Partial | Playing and being coached is visible; "differently" from Yousician is not |
| T2 | Judge says "not a tutor" in 15 seconds | Fail | "AI personalises lessons to your level" is an AI music tutor; Yousician sells "your AI guitar teacher" (3) |
| T3 | Structural answer to "why can't I just use Claude?" | Partial | Real-time listening and finger tracking beat a chatbot, but not Yousician, Simply or ROLI |
| T4 | One named user at one moment, with a phrase | Partial | User is "any student without access"; no single person or moment yet |
| T5 | Student thinks more; AI never answers the deliverable | Pass | Practice is effortful; AI never generates music, which matches the 68% vs 18% split (71) |
| T6 | Mapped to unit learning outcomes | Fail | Hobby framing, no unit; passes only with a university anchor (79, 77) |
| T7 | Rides on what universities run; no device access | Partial | Needs phone camera and microphone and video of hands; no campus platform used |
| T8 | Zero added teacher burden | Pass | Self-directed practice; a lecturer only validates a record if reframed |
| T9 | Flips the metric | Fail | Still scores notes hit, like every incumbent; no shift to practice quality or readiness |
| T10 | One path live in 60 seconds | Partial | Microphone pitch is reliable; camera chords need staged lighting and a fixed angle (58, 62) |
| T11 | One number from a real test that week | Partial | Feasible: peers learn three chords with and without feedback; needs guitars on hand |
| T12 | Costed pilot at a sponsor university plus inclusion lever | Partial | Inclusion lever is strong; no sponsor unit named; teacher education is the natural fit |

- Score: 2 pass, 7 partial, 3 fail (T2, T6, T9); past winners cleared these three (research synthesis).
- "Why can't I just use Claude or ChatGPT?":
  o Against a chatbot the answer is structural: it does not score pitch, timing and finger position live while you play.
  o The judge's real question is "why not Yousician, Simply or ROLI?", which already listen live at scale (1, 5, 40).
  o The only structural answers left are a course-mapped, student-owned practice record or a cohort layer; neither is in the pitch.

## 9. Verdict

- Occupied: yes, heavily, on lessons, live scoring and now AI teachers (1, 3, 40).
  o Yousician claims 20M+ monthly users; Yousician June and ROLI's coach already sell AI teaching.
  o A playable phone erhu and pipa have shipped in GarageBand since 2016 (81).
- Real gap: narrow, and not "access to lessons".
  o Phone-camera guitar coaching is unshipped and still research-grade (52, 62).
  o Culturally specific instruments have only tuners, notation and video (32 to 36).
  o No product found ties practice to a university unit outcome with a record the student owns.
- Jeff's "nobody else thought of" rule: fails as proposed.
  o "Personalised AI music lessons on your phone" is a better version of Yousician, Simply and ROLI (3, 4, 40).
  o It passes only if the user and the angle change.
- Stanley's narrowing: one instrument and one learner, decided now.
  o Guitar open chords suit microphone and camera checks in a demo (58).
  o Drop "every instrument" and cultural preservation; First Nations consent cannot be secured by 11 Nov (75).
- Hobby-app risk: high as pitched.
  o Anchors exist: ANU's beginner guitar unit (79); pre-service teachers get about 8 music hours (77).
  o 82% of surveyed NSW primary teachers called their music training inadequate (76).
- Recommendation: reframe; do not chase as proposed, do not park.
  o Strongest reframe: a practice studio for pre-service primary teachers learning to teach music (76, 77, 84).
  o The phone listens and watches, never plays; each session adds to a student-owned record mapped to the unit outcome.
  o It slots into the gamified journey, keeps the team's music energy, and answers T6, T9 and T12.
