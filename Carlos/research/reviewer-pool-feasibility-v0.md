# Reviewer pool feasibility v0: can the hand-off reviewer pool exist, who supplies it, and what we may say

- Date: overnight 6 to 7 Nov 2026 (AEDT).
- Purpose: test whether the reviewer pool that idea-bank-v1 s2 says is the hard-to-copy part can exist in 2027.
- Scope: supply options, rules, the review packet, pilot capacity and cost, honest wording. Proposals only; nobody has agreed to anything.
- Status: overnight draft for the team; verify before the pitch. The GPT-6 Sol critique was folded in on 7 Nov (see Method).

## 1. Bottom line
- Can it exist? Not by Term 1 2027 (1 Feb). Term 2 (from 28 Apr) at the earliest. That needs a university to agree by about February.
  o Term 1 is ruled out by lead times. An ACT WWVP check takes about 8 weeks on average, and over 60 working days if a risk assessment is needed [1].
  o Ethics, and possibly Directorate approval, also come before it (customer-commitment-pack-v0 s5: Directorate about 10 weeks).
  o University students are on summer break from Nov to Feb, so recruiting must wait for Semester 1 (`unverified` dates).
  o Term 2 is possible only if the reviewers already hold a check, or apply in early March. Mid-Term 2 is the realistic start.
- Who would supply it? Lean: supervised university students recruited through a university outreach team that already runs student mentors.
  o Use the existing outreach model, not a new teacher-education unit. Precedents (all exist): In2science volunteers (La Trobe is a partner university) [2][4]; UC pays casual student outreach staff (Aspiration Agents, snippet) [7].
  o Pre-service primary teachers fit the story ("practice they lack"), but their music training is thin. Fading Notes: about 8 hours of music in a primary degree (state-of-play s5).
  o Music performance students (ANU Bachelor of Music) know music better but have no teaching training. ANU is not a sponsor [8].
- Under what rule? Three conditions. The rules a page states are in section 3; the rest are our proposals:
  o Every reviewer holds a check in the learner's state. ACT volunteers register free; paid work costs $163 [1].
  o A qualified music educator approves every reply.
  o Contact happens only inside a logged, moderated channel, as In2science and Big Brothers Big Sisters do [4][5].
  o Gap found on 7 Nov: the ACT Child Safe Standards cover "all providers of services for children", music lessons and tutoring included [17].
  o So someone must be the provider. Before recruiting, the host signs off the role, checks, consent, clip access, reporting route and responsible officer.
  o Universities sit outside the ACT Reportable Conduct Scheme, but schools are inside it (snippet, 2018 fact sheet) [19]. A pilot run through ACT schools may bring those duties in (`unverified`).
- What the pitch may say now (22 words, proposed): "Proposed: when the coach can't help, a supervised, child-safety-checked university student hears your clip; pilot target, a reply in three working days."
- The one fact that would change the answer: a sponsor's outreach or teacher-education team says in writing that it will host 3 to 5 reviewers inside an existing programme whose students already hold checks.
  o That removes the 8-week check lead time and turns "proposed" into "agreed in principle".

## 4. What a review is
- The packet (one case, one screen; handoff-design-v1 s3 card fields and s4 "one screen"):
  o Clip: one 10 to 30 second take the learner chose; streamed, no download.
  o Goal: the learner's goal sentence and the small goal (take-data-contract s1.5 Plan).
  o Fixes tried: each cue's cause, fix and proof, with before-and-after numbers (take-data-contract s1.4 Cue).
  o Cue history and trigger: why the case opened, in plain words (handoff-design-v1 s2 T1 to T5).
  o Header: case id, "Learner 12", instrument, age band, state, days to expiry; no name or contact details.
- The reviewer's five-minute task (planning figure 5 plus 1 supervisor minute, an assumption in handoff-design-v1 s7):
  o Minute 1: read the goal and the fixes tried.
  o Minutes 2 to 3: play the clip twice.
  o Minutes 4 to 5: write one reply, or tap "Cannot assess"; no duty to invent a cue.
- The reply format, in plain words (proposal):
  o One kind thing, then one fix (social-and-fear-evidence-v1 SF5): "Your start was steady. Next: breathe before the high note, not on it."
  o One sentence, external focus; or a 30-second voice note; or "suggest a lesson" (booked by the programme); or "cannot assess".
  o The supervisor approves it before the learner sees it. The retake shows whether the next take improved, not that the cue caused it.
- Escalation when a reviewer sees a safeguarding concern (handoff-design-v1 s6.1):
  o Do not reply, ask the child anything, or contact the family; note the case id and the exact words.
  o Press "Raise a concern"; tell the supervisor the same day; the child-safety officer triages and reports; 000 for immediate danger.
  o If the officer or supervisor is involved or unreachable, go to the university backup or the external authority directly.

## 5. Cost and capacity arithmetic
- Hand-off rate: no team file states one. business-case-v1 s3c line 1 assumes hours of teacher time per learner, not cases. So the rates below are our estimates.
  o Low: 1 shared case per learner per 12 weeks. Mid: 3. High: 6 (one every 6 of 36 sessions; sessions from business-case-v1 3b).
- Time per case: 5 reviewer minutes plus 2 supervisor minutes (screen 1, approve 1). Estimate; handoff-design-v1 s7 plans 5 plus 1.
- Two ceilings: 2 reviewer hours a week each (brief), and a cap of about 10 cases per reviewer a week (handoff-design-v1 s5, assumption).

- Arithmetic: cases a week = cases total / 12. Reviewer hours = cases a week x 5 / 60. Supervisor hours = cases a week x 2 / 60. Round reviewer counts up.
- Add one backup reviewer for reply-time cover. So 3 reviewers cover the core pilot at mid rate, and 5 cover the full pilot at mid rate.
  o This matches handoff-design-v1's shape of one supervisor and 5 to 10 reviewers.
