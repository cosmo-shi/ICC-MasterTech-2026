# Free and low-cost music programs: partner map

- Date: 6 to 7 Nov 2026 (AEDT). Purpose: answer "free programs with real instruments exist, why not go there" and name two programs to approach.
- Scope: Australian programs giving beginners an instrument or a teacher; NSW, Vic and ACT rows filled from program pages; Yousician is an app, not a program (one-line note in section 5).
- Status: overnight draft for the team; verify before the pitch. Critique pass from Codex folded (see Method).
- Bottom line: say "go there; the app is home practice between sessions and rides inside such a program".
  o Approach the ACT Instrumental Music Program first and Music in Me (Australian Youth Orchestra) second.
  o Just Brass (Salvation Army) is the runner-up.
  o Only the ACT IMP page we opened writes home practice into its model (parents give a space and time, daily practice journal); no opened page mentions a home-practice tool. The gap is an opening, not proven demand.
  o Hard fact for the team: most of these programs teach wind, brass or percussion; our demo is keyboard or voice. Fit depends on that (section 2).

## 2. Fit table
Fit = how well the app complements the program, not whether the program will say yes.

| Program | What the app adds inside it | What it competes with | Risk | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Music in Me | A short practice loop for the class between mentor visits, so the teacher is not the only feedback | Nothing; it trains the teacher, students get no instrument feedback | Program is about teacher skill; a student app is off its brief; schools pay fees, so a new tool needs a budget line | Medium: audience matches (regional, remote) but the model is teachers, not students |
| Just Brass | Practice between weekly band rehearsals for a child who has the instrument at home | The band leader as the only listener | Our demo is keyboard or voice, not brass; small local-volunteer teams; some sites may not allow take-home | Medium: clearest home-practice gap but instrument mismatch |
| ACT IMP | Feedback on the practice the program already asks parents to support (home space, daily practice journal), for a lent instrument, Year 5 and 6 | The paper practice journal and the visiting teacher; group of about 23 leaves little individual feedback | Government department; privacy and under-15 rules; schools may pass costs to families so "free" needs care; wind, brass and percussion, not keyboard or voice | High on the model (home practice is written into it), medium on the product (instrument mismatch) |
| Qld IMP / WA IMSS | Practice help in sparse regional weeks; WA and Qld both pick on practice commitment | The itinerant teacher and school ensemble | Selective entry means the app helps already-selected students, not the ones without access | Low: serves the already-selected |

## 3. The two to approach
- Update after Codex critique and a check of the ACT IMP Year 5 and 6 page [21]:
  o ACT IMP is the strongest evidence of a home-practice gap (loan form, practice journal, parents asked to support practice).
  o Pick order is now ACT IMP first and Music in Me second; Just Brass is runner-up.
  o Treat every "gap" below as an unverified hypothesis until a program leader answers.
- First: ACT IMP (ACT Education Directorate).
  o Why: Year 5 and 6 matches the pitch picture, Canberra, instrument lent, and the program already asks families for regular home practice and a daily journal [21].
  o Write to: the IMP principal, by role (enquiries line and email on the ACT pages [7][9]).
  o Risk: departmental approval and under-15 privacy; wind, brass and percussion instruments, not keyboard or voice; no answer likely before 11 Nov.
- Second (old first): Music in Me, run by the Australian Youth Orchestra.
  o Why: it targets regional, remote and lower ICSEA primary schools, as does our learner, and it already trains the person who runs the lesson.
  o Write to: the program director, or the program manager for the school's state (NSW or Vic), by role.
  o Risk: it serves teachers, not students.
- Runner-up (old second): Just Brass, the Salvation Army.
  o Why: free lessons, a supplied instrument (take-home at some sites) and a weekly band leave the learner alone between rehearsals; the gap is concrete.
  o Write to: a local Just Brass program leader, or the national Just Brass contact, by role.
  o Risk: brass is not our demo; a pitch-detection phrase on brass is unverified.
- Runner-up for Canberra: ACT IMP, through the program principal; matches Year 5 and the city, but a departmental sign-off is unlikely before 11 Nov.
- The four-sentence ask, in plain words:
  o "Your learners practise alone between sessions, and the teacher cannot hear it."
  o "We are building a phone app that listens to a short phrase and gives one thing to fix."
  o "Would you tell us, in one line, whether that gap is real for your learners?"
  o "What do your learners do today when they get stuck at home?"
- Ask by Wednesday: nothing but a one-line answer to the third and fourth sentences.
  o One reply is one contact's view, not demand or a partnership, and we cannot quote it without written permission.
  o No quote, no phrase set, no partnership, no pilot (a test is a later conversation).
  o A phrase set from a program teacher is a later ask (sunday-decisions-sheet row A18 proposal; partner unconfirmed).

## 4. The honest stage sentence and the never-say list
- Stage sentence (18 words): "A prototype of one practice loop; no program has agreed to anything; we would like to test it."
- Never say:
  o "We partner with ..." or "Music in Me / Just Brass / any program is our partner" (no program has been contacted or has agreed).
  o "First", "nobody has done this", "no one else".
  o "Free programs fail" or "programs do not work"; say they run with a teacher and the app covers practice at home.
  o Any figure from a program page without the date and source; counts here are from a program's own page or marked snippet.
  o "Music in Me students" as our learners; it mentors teachers.
