# Uniqueness evidence v1: what makes the coach succeed, and what we can honestly call different

- Date: Fri 6 Nov 2026 night (AEDT); all pages accessed 2026-11-06 unless stated.
- Purpose: external evidence for each part of the combination, why music apps win or lose, who else offers it, and three stage-safe claims.
- Scope: learning-science base, retention and review data, combination check, Australian access angle; does not redo the 21-app cards in competitor-feedback-mechanics-v1.md.
- Status: overnight draft for the team; verify before the pitch. Codex critique folded (see Method).
- Bottom line: no single part is new; the evidence supports the combination plus the outreach channel, not exclusivity.
  o Each part has support: feedback that is specific, reduced and re-tested; small goals with planning support; humans plus AI. Sources are mostly reviews and one-off studies, not music trials of our design.
  o Defensibility is delivery, not features: the channel, mentor capacity and cohort, not the five visible steps (7 Nov, after the MakeMusic and Yousician pages were opened).
  o The nearest product found is Mastery: AI Audio Coach (scored breakdown, retake, human reviewer notes by link). Say "combination", never "only".
  o Biggest evidence gap: the retake proves a short-term fix, not retained learning. Add a delayed, unaided retake to the demo story and the pilot.

## 1. Evidence per component

| Component | What the evidence says | Strength | Source | What it does not show |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| One fix at a time | Feedback is among the major influences on learning but effect depends on its type and delivery; praise and reward weaker (Hattie and Timperley 2007, via search extract) | Strong source, secondary extract | 10 (snippet) | Does not test one item against two or three |
| One fix at a time | In 17 pianists, how accurately they practised, not how much, tracked next-day performance (Duke, Simmons and Cash 2009, via a summary) | Moderate, small n, summary only | 8 (snippet) | One passage, next day only |
| One fix at a time | Guzheng narrative review 2026: segmented practice, worked examples with fading support, calibrated feedback look promising; instrument-specific evidence limited | Weak to moderate, narrative review | 5 (opened record) | Not our instruments or an app |
| One fix at a time | Vocal-feedback review 2026 (36 studies): a trade-off between cognitive load and dependence on guidance | Moderate, systematic review | 3 (opened record) | Pitch displays, not plain-word fixes |
| Proof on the retake | Motor learning: feedback on about two-thirds of trials gave better later, unaided accuracy than every-trial feedback in Parkinson's patients (2010) and rats (2020); a 1999 timing study found no benefit from reduced frequency | Moderate, mixed, non-music tasks | 2 (opened record, summaries) | Says "retake after every try" may be too much; proof should be paced |
| Proof on the retake | Salmoni, Schmidt and Walter 1984 is the standard review separating knowledge of results from knowledge of performance (Psychological Bulletin 95:355-386) | Record opened, abstract not returned | 1 (opened record) | Content of the distinction is `unverified (Codex knowledge)` here |
| Proof on the retake | Vocal review: spectral feedback linked to self-efficacy gains; the metacognition-to-skill link tested null; 29 of 36 studies did not test the feedback-to-skill pathway | Moderate | 3 (opened record) | The retake builds confidence more surely than skill |
| Proof on the retake | Piano AR evidence map 2026: strongest for immediate outcomes; delayed unaided transfer "weakly tested" | Moderate, evidence map | 5 (opened record) | Same gap in the whole field |
| Small goals | Systematic review of 16 studies: ordinary digital tools supported planning and reflection; AI error detection supported real-time correction; "over-scaffolding that may diminish autonomy" understudied; authors suggest fading support | Moderate, uneven quality | 3 (opened record) | No causal trial |
| Small goals | 458 Chinese music students: self-efficacy linked to deliberate practice partly through achievement goal orientation | Weak, correlational survey | 4 (opened record) | Not goal size or splitting |
| Small goals | Planning, progress monitoring and commitment numbers | Already verified | procrastination-small-goals-evidence.md (folder) | Not redone here |

| Human hand-off | Tutor CoPilot: 900 tutors, 1,800 under-served students; tutors with AI support had students 4 points more likely to master topics, 9 points for lower-rated tutors; about US$20 per tutor a year | Moderate, preprint, randomised design per abstract | 7 (opened) | AI helps the human; ours sends the learner to the human |
| Human hand-off | Review of 40 intelligent tutoring studies in real classrooms: mixed results, weak designs in some | Moderate | 6 (opened record) | Not music |
| Human hand-off | Stiftung Warentest (9 Jun 2025): five apps tested; they flag wrong notes but missed off-beat playing; none gave feedback on expression, posture or handling; personal lessons "still best" | Moderate, independent consumer test, no scores published | 14 (opened) | A verdict, not an outcome study |

- Honest reading: each component has support; none has a music trial of our design.
  o We cannot cite a study that says "exactly one fix" or "hand off at a ceiling" works. Present both as design choices backed by the principles above.
  o Tightened (second Astra fold): the sources give principles, not hypotheses proven for us. Feedback research sets no best item count, and goal orientation is not goal size.
  o Evidence comes from different tasks and ages; none tests the combined loop. Treat "one fix" as a design bet to compare against a list with beginners of the target age.

## 2. Why apps win or lose

| Source | Finding | Type and strength | Reading for us |
|---|---|---|---|
| Playground Sessions CEO in Built In, 15 Mar 2020 | "Nine out of 10 people who pick up a new instrument quit within 60 days"; the same piece reports 50% retention after 18 months, with 20,000 to 30,000 monthly users | CEO quote, no method, 2020 | Early dropout is the problem; do not quote as a rate (13, opened) |
| Same article | Developer: polyphonic pitch detection "starts failing"; mono pitch "super simple" | Insider quote | One short phrase is the technically safe scope (13, opened) |
| Sensor Tower blog, Oct 2023 | Simply Piano churn 64% vs Duolingo 28% (Western markets); 33% retained; churn undefined, no window | Analytics firm, undefined, 3 years old | Shows scale of churn; not a clean number (12, opened) |
| Stiftung Warentest, 9 Jun 2025 | Apps limited; only flowkey convinced on piano; all four guitar apps had clear weaknesses; apps miss off-beat playing | Independent test | Judges can hear this from a neutral source (14, opened) |
| Trustpilot, Yousician (1,322 reviews, 3.9; 80% five-star, 9% one-star) | First 23 reviews shown: 10 about billing or cancellation (9 complaints), 8 about content or progress, 4 about app or support, 1 about feedback accuracy ("misses fret 5 on low E") | Self-selected reviews; theme counts from the page extract, recount before quoting | Complaints are mostly money, not teaching (15, opened) |
| Trustpilot, Simply Piano (12 reviews in 12 months, 2.2; 11 one-star) | 8 of 11 shown about charges or cancelling; one says the app "doesn't recognise piano notes" | Tiny sample | Same pattern; sample too small to generalise (16, opened) |
| Trustpilot, flowkey (422 reviews, 4.0; 43% five-star) | First 23: most about song choice or content; 3 about notes misread or not registered; praise for wait mode and fingering guidance; one asks for error feedback outside wait mode | Self-selected; page extract | Beginners praise visible, specific guidance and complain when listening is wrong (17, opened) |

- Caution (second Astra fold): reviewers are not verified beginners, opening pages reflect selection and order, and billing complaints do not explain learning failure or churn.
  o Read the counts as what unhappy or keen customers write, not what beginners need. Target-user interviews are the test (Open questions).
- What reviewers wrote, by the counts above: visible progress, step-by-step structure, guidance that names the finger or note, the songs they care about.
  o What they hate: surprise charges and hard cancellation first; wrong "listening" second.
  o Design consequences: free or clearly priced learner version (university-funded), a visible "wrong note flagged because" explanation, no auto-renewal.
- Teachers: Warentest and Built In quote none. One small survey opened: 33 piano teachers (Sochor 2020, University of Wisconsin-Stevens Point thesis); "a large percentage" use apps for note and rhythm work, opinions on usefulness "differ" (28, opened abstract, no percentages).
  o Search snippet only: small Turkish samples (16 teachers; n=100 students) with limited app use (29, snippet). Teacher voice is thin, say so.
- Retention data: no clean published retention curve found for Yousician, flowkey or Simply; all figures above are undefined or vendor-sourced. Do not state a churn rate on stage.

- Found: products with retake tracking and human review exist (Mastery, SmartMusic, PracticeFirst, Between Lessons, ArtistWorks); our pieces are not each absent from the market.
  o Pattern, tightened: ArtistWorks supplies its own teachers; the others expect the learner to bring one. No opened page describes an institution supplying the mentor, but page silence is not absence.
  o Mastery sells on iOS alone per its developer page (35, opened); regions and institutions not stated there.
  o What this search did not find: a page that states all four together (explained single fix, retake proof, ceiling hand-off, funded outreach channel). Absence in a short search is not proof of absence.
  o Not checked: app store listings beyond the above, paid products behind logins, non-English products.
- Safest framing: "the combination, aimed at learners without a teacher, through universities".

- Never say "only", "first" or "no one else"; say "combination".
