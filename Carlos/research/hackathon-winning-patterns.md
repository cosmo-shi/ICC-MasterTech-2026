## 2. How winners read the question

- Winners rewrite a broad question as "who, stuck at what moment, changes how".
  o Alcademy narrowed "smart education" to one behaviour: academic procrastination.
  o PlanRoadmap narrowed productivity to ADHD task paralysis.
- Winners move the user one step from the obvious student-with-chatbot.
  o Dyscover served teachers; EmpowerIEP served parents; FABULA served teachers of language-delayed pupils.
  o Wharton judges named this move: "beyond the typical ed-tech approach".
- Winners pick a learner the mainstream product ignores, then name them exactly.
  o APD children (TAWI), blind students (Wonder Reader), dyslexic readers (KOBI), cognitive disability (Vite Vere).
- Winners take the verb in the question literally.
  o "Learn" became practise and explain (Maieutic, MedKit), not "receive answers".
- Winners read the sponsor as the customer.
  o Ray Deck's test: "If I reward this project, will my company look good?"
  o Cisco D-1 Grand Prix used Meraki and MCP inside a school, with CO2 tonnes per year.
- Winners attach a number the judge can repeat.
  o Super FNETS: ~3.5 t plus ~6 t CO2 per year for a 50-access-point school.
- Winners make the challenge link explicit, because judges may see the entry in isolation.
  o GovHack rules: video must explain the project "in isolation"; criterion = "relevance to the nominated challenge".
- Winners cut scope until one end-to-end path runs.
  o Kurbaitaev: "Ambition is not the signal. Judgment under a constraint is the signal."
- Winners prepare for "why is this better than what exists?" before judges ask.
  o Sentinel Hack judge rejected an idea with exactly that question (holygrimm, DEV 2024).
- Winners in effect pass five tests; use them to filter ideas before building.
  o Does the one-line identity name a user other than "students"?
  o Would a judge say "not a tutor" within 15 seconds?
  o Can one end-to-end path run live in 60 seconds?
  o Is there one number from a real test this week?
  o Would Cisco or a sponsor university look good rewarding it?

## 3. How judges actually score uniqueness

- Originality is a point of view, not a new invention.
  o HackathonHost: "a distinct point of view, an unexpected solution, or a smart combination of familiar ideas".
- Novel framing beat novel tech in the clearest education win found.
  o Dyscover used Teachable Machine and Gradio; judges rewarded the reframe and rigour (Wharton 2025).
- Judges compare you with the room, not with an ideal.
  o Kurbaitaev: "Judges are not evaluating your project against an ideal."
  o Implication: with three tutor apps in the room, a fourth tutor scores as the fourth tutor.
- Tutor entries place but rarely win in the cases checked.
  o AAAI-25 tutor placed 3rd; Cisco Malaysia "AI Private Tutor" placed 3rd (Search only).
- Stack choice does not earn uniqueness points.
  o Kurbaitaev: "Nobody scored higher for a stack."
- Novelty is often a tie-breaker, weighted below severity and feasibility.
  o ai-beavers (Oct 2026): novelty "should not outrank problem severity, adoption likelihood, feasibility".
- Rubrics pair originality with relevance and impact, never alone.
  o GovHack: originality, relevance to challenge, social value, quality, usability.
  o Cisco D-1: Cisco tech, originality and forward vision, social impact, UI/UX, business potential.
  o Imagine Cup 2025: innovative AI use, inclusive design, overall viability.
- AI must be the core of the value, not a bolt-on.
  o Aderant rubric scores low when AI is "tacked on"; asks "core part of the value, or a gimmick?" (Search only).
- Memorability is scored implicitly: can the judge retell it later?
  o Kurbaitaev: winners have "a one-line identity"; YC lists "Memorable" as a core quality.
- Relevance protects against impressive but off-brief builds.
  o HackathonHost: relevance "prevents an impressive but unrelated build from overtaking a project that solved the actual brief".

## 4. The 3-minute pitch that wins

| Seconds | Beat | Example line (illustrative) | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 to 15 | What it is, for whom, in one sentence | "X helps first-year engineering students prove they understand a lab before they touch the kit." | Kurbaitaev: open with what and for whom; YC: say what you do early |
| 15 to 35 | One named user at the moment it fails | "Priya, week 3, copies a config that works and cannot explain why it broke." | Kurbaitaev: "Who is this for, specifically?" |
| 35 to 50 | The reframe: why the obvious answer is wrong | "Three apps answer her questions. None checks that she learned." | Wharton: "beyond the typical ed-tech approach" |
| 50 to 110 | Live demo, one end-to-end path, no slides | Show the student explaining, the AI challenging, the lecturer view updating | MLH: "a demo of their hack, not a presentation"; Kurbaitaev: working software wins |
| 110 to 130 | Evidence | "We tested with 9 students this week: 6 of 9 found a gap they did not know." | Colosseum: show user feedback, even informal; Startup Weekend: customer validation (Search only) |
| 130 to 150 | Business case on one slide | "Faculty pays per unit per term; one lecturer replaces N hours of marking checks." | Startup Weekend: business model (Search only); YC: under seven words per slide |
| 150 to 170 | What it takes to proceed | "One unit, one semester, two tutors, ethics approval: we measure pass and retention." | Taikai: realistic for resources, timeline, scale |
| 170 to 180 | Close with the line to remember | "Not AI that answers students. AI that makes students answer." | YC: end "with a bang", say what to remember |

- Rehearse the first 30 seconds aloud; Kurbaitaev: "That is where the score is set."
- Keep a 60-second screen recording as demo insurance against venue Wi-Fi.
- Name your known weakness when asked; silence "lose[s] credibility instantly" (Kurbaitaev).

## 5. Losing patterns

- Opening with market size, a personal story, or a long problem statement.
  o Judges spent a third of the slot not knowing what they watched (Kurbaitaev).
- Slides instead of working software.
  o "Slide decks. They fill time. They do not persuade." (Kurbaitaev); MLH wants a demo, not a presentation.
- A broad platform in place of one narrow, finished path.
  o Narrow, well-built projects outscored broad platforms (Kurbaitaev); Ray Deck: teams "made big claims, and didn't deliver".
- A vague user that a judge cannot picture.
  o "Athletes" lost points to specific answers (Kurbaitaev); Growth Garden is the exception, saved by design.
- No answer to "what improvement over existing standards?"
  o Sentinel Hack team had to abandon its idea three hours before the deadline (holygrimm).
- Picking the topic you like instead of the sponsor's interest.
  o Ray Deck's team chose web3 for a Hellosign prize and lost to an AI entry.
- AI bolted onto an ordinary app.
  o Rubrics mark down "...and we added a chatbot" (Aderant, Search only); Reddit complaints about wrapper wins (Search only).
- Ignoring cost and scale questions judges will ask.
  o Brisk Steer judges flagged citywide scale and traffic-light hardware cost; it did not win.

## 6. Applied to 2026: "How might we use AI and emerging technology to transform the way students learn and succeed?"

- Teach-back gate: AI makes the student explain before it helps, then grades the explanation.
  o Pattern: Maieutic, MedKit. Answers "learn" literally; evidence = explanation quality before and after.
- Lecturer early-warning: spot a hidden learning difference or disengagement from work students already submit.
  o Pattern: Dyscover. User = lecturer or tutor; "succeed" = fewer silent fails, measured by retention.
- Navigator for the person beside the student: parent, carer, or support officer.
  o Pattern: EmpowerIEP. Example user: families of first-in-family students decoding university systems.
- Practice simulator scored on reasoning for hands-on degrees.
  o Pattern: MedKit. Example: AI examiner injects faults into a simulated network lab for Cisco Academy students.
- One behaviour at one moment, not "all of learning".
  o Pattern: Alcademy, PlanRoadmap. Example: the first 48 hours after an assignment is released.
- Sponsor tech inside a learning space, with a number.
  o Pattern: Super FNETS. Example: campus network signals show which labs students use; report hours, not opinions.
- A named under-served learner, on everyday hardware.
  o Pattern: TAWI, Wonder Reader, Yiya. Example: regional students on low bandwidth, a gap judges probed at Cisco D-1.
- Proof of learning instead of cheating detection.
  o Pattern: Maieutic. User = marker; the student's process becomes the evidence, answering "succeed" fairly.
- AI that groups students stuck on the same thing, so peers teach peers.
  o Pattern: HiveMind (Search only). Differs from tutors: the AI routes people, it does not answer.
- Success beyond grades: turn coursework into evidenced skills an employer trusts.
  o Pattern: Wingspans (Tools Competition 2024). User = final-year student plus industry partner; sponsor universities care.
