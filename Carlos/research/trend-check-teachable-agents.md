## 3. Uniqueness verdict

- The concept is not new: teachable agents run from Betty's Brain (2009) to five LLM tutee systems in 2023 to 2026.
  o Axiom Flow, Chrysalis, Explique, MatlabTutee and Socrates all let university students teach an AI.
- Nearest product is Axiom Flow: live at Peradeniya, Sam sits an exam on what he was taught.
  o Teachers see full transcripts and Sam's remaining misconceptions per student; students see ideas that did not land.
  o Traction is thin: about 100 students, self-reported vendor survey, Product Hunt numbers 1 and 3.
- No page I read shows a per-learning-outcome mastery map that the student owns as a private progress map.
  o Axiom Flow and Protege route logs and estimates to teachers; data ownership is not stated.
  o Cohort-level shared misconceptions were not described in any product read; absence is from searches, not proof.
- No Australian student-teaches-AI pilot found; Cogniti pilots at Sydney and Melbourne are educator-built tutor and role-play agents.
- Honest claim: novel combination (outcome map, student ownership, cohort view), not a novel idea; "first" would be unsafe.
  o Learning evidence is thin: most samples 20 to 41, mostly preprints, one 96-person trial showed no knowledge gain.

## 4. What would make ours different

- Student owns the record: teaching log and mastery map are private by default, shared with staff only by choice.
  o Axiom Flow and Protege both send logs or estimates to the teacher.
- Mastery is mapped per unit learning outcome, not per uploaded document or lesson.
  o The apprentice attempts the week's real problem using only what the student explained.
  o The failing step is traced to the missing or wrong explanation.
- Cohort patterns are shown to staff as anonymous aggregates of shared misconceptions, with a minimum group size.
  o No product or paper read describes this view.
- Design against known failure modes found in the sources.
  o LLM drops its novice role (Chrysalis); apprentice must stay inside what was taught.
  o Agent learns shallowly or student teaches only easy items (SimStudent, Search only).
  o Copy-paste explanations: block pasting as Axiom Flow does.
  o Little practice at fixing errors when the agent is mostly right (Chen et al.).
- Test honestly: pre and post with a control, and report null results; claim only what the pilot shows.
  o Published effects are small-sample; the largest field study gave no effect sizes.
