## 2. Pre-2024: the school pilots that shaped the format

### The question as asked

- 2022 Ivanhoe cohort got a "mystery box of technological ingredients" (Read).
  o Brief: "use as many ingredients as you can and show us a new world" (Read).
- 2023 cohort built apps and games in Godot, Unity, JoinXR and Microsoft HoloLens (Read).

### How the organisers framed it

- Co-run by Steve Brophy (Ivanhoe) and Prof Jeff Jones, Director, Cisco Innovation Central Melbourne (Read).
- Based on Jeff Jones's book "Collective Social Intelligence", scaled for 20-30 students (Read, Cisco blog).
- Cisco blog calls it "MasterChef with technology", with a "pantry" of tools (Read).

### What teams built

| Team | Solution | Pitch in one line | Tech | Cisco fit | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ivanhoe 2022 students | Holograms, gesture games, 3D worlds, interactive dinner tables, live speech-to-text | Show a new world with the ingredients given | Not itemised | None stated | Expo at the hub, "packed to the rafters" |
| ICM example project | "Jenny" | AI tool guiding visually impaired people, for example lift over stairs | AI | None stated | Cited as a showcase example |
| ICM example project | Holographic sign language | Real-time sign interpretation for deaf people | Webex, Microsoft HoloLens | Webex | Cited as a showcase example |

### What organisers said

- "the product didn't matter as much as the learning" (Read, Ivanhoe 2022).
- ICM: "Teams are not eliminated, but often the details of their ideas are" (Read).

### The standard MasterTech week (ICM case study, Read)

| Day | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1-3 | Try new things in any language; 3D models, animations, virtual worlds; fail fast, pivot, several prototypes |
| Hidden twist | At least one technology element is not introduced at the start; it may be in the room, broken or unopened |
| 4 | "Surprising task" meant to move ideas forward and shape a positive story |
| Final day | Finish and showcase like a commercial expo or trade show |
| Deliverable | "A dynamic three-minute video narrative of their journey" plus a final pitch on exhibition day |

### What made it work (Analysis)

- Organisers reward visible learning and pivots, not polish alone.
- Hidden ingredient tests whether teams notice and fold in new kit fast.
- Expo style means judges walk up; the demo must explain itself in seconds.

## 3. 2024: Coles sustainable store of the future

### The question as asked

- ICB (Read): Coles asked "What would you want to see in a Coles sustainable store of the future?"
- ICA title (Read): "Reimagining the Sustainable Store of the Future".
- UC (Read): teams were to "design experiences around sustainable stores of the future".
- Winning student (Read, UC): "The challenge was about designing a world you want to engage in".

### How the sponsor framed it

- Coles mission quoted: "Helping all Australians live and eat healthier, happier lives" (Read, ICB).
- Focus areas: waste, energy, sustainable sourcing, eco packaging, community engagement (Read, ICB).
- Coles asked teams to think "as consumers and suppliers" (Read, EcoVoice).
- Coles kept "the option to implement any of the ideas developed" (Read, EcoVoice).
- Stake: winners "may earn the chance to collaborate directly with Coles Group" (Read, ICA).

### EcoSwaps detail

- Example: swap four small Coke bottles for two 1.25 L bottles; less plastic, lower cost (Read, RetailBiz).
- Built to "easily integrate with the existing Coles app infrastructure" (Read, UC).
- "an algorithm that can easily be implemented in the Coles website and app" (Read, RetailBiz).

### What organisers and judges said

- Nick Walker, Coles: "really impressed with how the students focused on solutions that were very feasible" (Read, UC).
- Coles staff impressed by "understanding of the challenge" and "feasible solutions in a short period" (Read, EcoVoice).
- Coles "acknowledged that the ideas were readily implementable" (Read, EcoVoice).
- Reg Johnson, Cisco, congratulated ICC on winning the inaugural program (Read, UC).
- No rubric or named judging panel published (Unverified).

### What happened after

- Cisco said it planned to strengthen its partnership with Coles (Read, UC).
- No EcoSwaps pilot or Coles rollout found (Unverified).

### What made the winner different (Analysis)

- One shopper action, one concrete example: the Coke bottle swap made the idea instantly clear.
- Rode on assets Coles already owns: the Coles app and Flybuys, so near-zero build risk.
- Win-win framing: less plastic and lower cost for the shopper, loyalty data for Coles.
- Small, feasible scope matched exactly what the Coles judge praised.

### What happened after

- $10,000 went to ICC to keep developing the idea (Read, UC).
- ICC Summer Showcase, 3 Mar 2026, listed "Walk & Give" from the Coles MasterTech hackathon (Read, NIIN).
  o Described as "rewarding customers for their time and engagement" in physical stores (Read).
  o Link between "Walk & Give" and the 2025 winning concept is my inference (Analysis).
- No Coles pilot found as of 5 Nov 2026 (Unverified).

### What made the winner different (Analysis)

- Named an emotional pain point in one memorable phrase: "walking wallets".
- Reframed the metric: reward presence and engagement, not spend, which flipped the sponsor's assumption.
- Added a social-impact lever (donate points to charity), matching the judges' "social impact" words.
- Personalisation came from data Coles already holds (past transactions), so feasible on day one.
- A playful mechanic (quest, treasure hunt) made the demo easy to show on a stand.

## 6. Patterns across editions

- Both winners came from Innovation Central Canberra, two years running (Read).
- Winners named one specific user in one moment: a shopper at the shelf or in the aisle.
  o 2024: shopper choosing a product; 2025: shopper walking the store.
- Winners plugged into a platform the sponsor already runs: Coles app, Flybuys, transaction history.
  o Judges' top word both years was feasibility or "readily implementable" (Read).
- Evidence was a concrete worked example, not a survey: the Coke bottle swap (Read).
- 2025 winner led with an emotional insight phrase ("walking wallets") before any technology.
- Winners flipped the sponsor's default metric: plastic saved (2024), presence over spend (2025).
- Social impact was built in: less plastic (2024), charity donations (2025); judges named it in 2025.
- AI was used quietly for personalisation, never as the headline: purchase history, AI quests.
- Neither winner reported a Cisco product; sponsor fit outweighed Cisco fit (Read; Analysis).
- "What next" was money and a path: Coles collaboration offer (2024), $10,000 to scale (2025).
- The format always includes a Day 4 surprise task and a three-minute journey video (Read, ICM).
- Pitch is live on the NIIN stand, trade-show style, to sponsor leaders and passers-by (Read).

## 7. Key insights for 2026

1. Pick one named student in one moment, for example a first-year IT student the night before a deadline.
  o Ties to: winners chose a single user at a single decision point.
2. Find the 2026 version of "walking wallets": students feel treated as a grade or a login count.
  o Ties to: the 2025 winner led with an emotional phrase, then the tech.
3. Remember the 2026 sponsors are universities plus Cisco; the judges own the student journey themselves.
  o Ties to: winners solved the sponsor's own operational problem, not a generic one.
4. Build on systems the universities already run: LMS (Moodle/Canvas), Webex, Cisco NetAcad, campus Wi-Fi.
  o Ties to: EcoSwaps rode on the Coles app; judges praised "readily implementable".
  o Cisco fit is a bonus: neither winner cited a Cisco product (Analysis).
5. Flip the default metric: reward effort, help-seeking or peer teaching, not marks or seat time.
  o Ties to: presence over spend (2025), plastic over price (2024).
6. Personalise from data the uni already holds, with student consent: attendance, LMS clicks, submissions.
  o Ties to: 2025 used past transactions; 2024 used purchase history.
7. Avoid the obvious "AI tutor chatbot"; most teams will pitch it, so make AI the engine, not the headline.
  o Ties to: neither winner sold AI as the product (Analysis).
  o Keep the design loose for the Day 4 surprise and the hidden pantry item.
8. Prepare one worked example a judge can repeat, like the Coke swap, with a before and after number.
  o Ties to: concrete example beat abstract claims in 2024.
9. Add a fairness or inclusion lever, for example support for first-in-family or remote students.
  o Ties to: "bold and inclusive" and "social impact" were the 2025 judges' words.
10. Close with a costed next step that matches the prize, for example a one-semester pilot at UC.
  o Ties to: 2025's $10,000 went to ICC to keep building; Walk & Give reappeared in March.

- Which student data may a prototype use: real LMS exports, synthetic data or none?
- What is in the 2026 pantry, and is a hidden ingredient again planned?
- What is the Day 4 surprise likely to target: user, budget, ethics or a new technology?
- Is the three-minute journey video still required, alongside the three-minute live pitch?
- Is there a prize or pilot path this year, like 2025's $10,000 to the hub?
