# Small goals and procrastination: evidence for and against

- Purpose: test the lead idea (AI splits unit work into small goals, student sees they are ahead) against the evidence.
- Date: 5 Nov 2026. Every source opened on 5 Nov 2026; anything not opened is marked "(unverified)".
- Habit, implementation intention, goal gradient and retrieval rows are not repeated: see gamification-research.md.

## 1. Procrastination among students: prevalence and cost

| Source | Sample | Finding | Effect size | Ref |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Steel 2007, Psychological Bulletin | 691 correlations | Strongest predictors: task aversiveness, task delay, self-efficacy, impulsiveness, low conscientiousness | Not on page | [1] |
| Rozental and Carlbring 2014 | Narrative review, self-report basis | About 15 to 20% of adults and about 50% of students report distressing procrastination | None | [2] |
| Kim and Seo 2015 | 33 studies, 38,529 people | Procrastination relates negatively to performance; self-report scales hid it; stronger in secondary school | r not obtained | [3] |
| Pooled meta-analysis, 1984 to 2018 studies | 30 studies | Higher procrastination, lower performance | -0.307 pooled (metric not stated) | [4] |
| Tunisian students | 1,019, medicine, law, engineering | Procrastination tracks failure and success | r = 0.22 with failure; r = -0.27 with success | [5] |
| Buehler, Griffin, Ross 1994 | 37 psychology students, thesis | Predicted 33.9 days; actual 55.5; best-case guess 27.4 | About 30% finished on time | [6] |
| Bisin and Hyndman | Students facing a fixed deadline | Present bias widespread; strong demand for self-set deadlines; students partly naive about their own self-control | Not on page | [12] |

- Temporal motivation theory: expectancy theory plus hyperbolic discounting; delay itself is the driver [1].
  o Design inference: bring the first reward and the first step near; a 12-week deadline feels far [1][8].
- The link to grades is real but modest, and weaker when procrastination is self-reported [3][4][5].
- The planning fallacy is the best "problem" fact: actual thesis time ran 64% over the prediction [6].
  o Eliminated when predictions were tied to past experience, per one summary of the 1994 paper (unverified).
- Australia: no peer-reviewed prevalence figure found; an industry survey ("four in five") is cited secondhand (unverified, do not pitch).
- The common "80 to 95% of students" figure traces to older sources quoted by Steel (unverified, do not pitch).
- Source [6] is a Wikipedia summary; the primary 1994 PDF is a scan I could not read.

## 2. Small goals, subgoals, deadlines and unfinished tasks

| Source | What was tested | Result | Caution | Ref |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bandura and Schunk 1981 | Children weak at maths: proximal subgoals vs distal goal vs none | Proximal: fast progress, mastery, self-efficacy, interest; distal: no measurable effect | Children; sample size not seen | [7] |
| Locke and Latham 2002 | 35 years of goal-setting research | Specific difficult goals beat easy, none or "do your best"; subgoals generally but not always supported; too many cut satisfaction | Read via a Wikipedia summary | [8] |
| Amabile and Kramer | 238 employees, 7 companies, about 12,000 diaries | Progress was the most prominent event on the best days | Workers, not students; self-report | [13] |
| Ariely and Wertenbroch 2002 | Self-imposed deadlines | RETRACTED: Europe PMC record carries pubType "Retracted Publication" and a retraction notice dated 2 Sep 2026; reanalysis reports edited grades; do not cite | Record re-checked by the session 5 Nov 2026 | [10][11] |
| Zeigarnik and Ovsiankina | Meta-analysis | No memory edge for unfinished tasks; general tendency to resume | Drop "unfinished tasks nag you" | [14] |
| Masicampo and Baumeister 2011 | Unfulfilled goals | A specific plan removed intrusive thoughts and the performance cost | Lab studies | [15] |
| Task segmentation (Forsyth and Burt 2008) | Subtask time estimates | Segmentation influential against planning fallacy but costly to do | Primary not opened (unverified) | [6] |
| Microlearning review 2024 | 40 studies, mostly medical and corporate | Positive on outcomes, not uniform | Not higher-education specific | [32] |
| Gao and Sakhnini 2025 | Optional unmarked early deadline with feedback, upper-year computing | More students started early; overall grades no different; early users scored higher | Selection effect likely | [16] |
| Aeon et al. 2021 | 158 studies, 53,000+ people | Time management helps academic success less than wellbeing; no effect on standardised tests | Effect sizes not on page | [18] |

> "starting early alone did not improve students' grades" [16]

- No trial found of AI-generated milestones from a unit outline and its effect on grades; this is the research gap.
- Deadline experiments in Israeli colleges and a Norwegian maths course appeared only in search summaries (unverified).
- Retire the deadline-precommitment evidence: its flagship study is retracted and the replication did not reproduce their effectiveness [10][11].

## 3. Nudges and reminders in higher education

| Study | Sample | Intervention | Result | Ref |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Oreopoulos et al. 2018 | 9,000+ students, 3 colleges | Weekly study-schedule module, text reminders or coach texts | A little more study time at two sites; precise nulls on credits, grades, retention | [19] |
| Dobronyi et al. 2017 | About 1,400 first-years | Two goal-setting exercises, half with reminders | No effect on GPA, credits, persistence; rules out about 0.07 SD | [20] |
| Bird et al. 2019 | 800,000+ students, 2 trials | Aid-form nudge campaigns | No effect on aid or enrolment; framing and timing made no difference | [21] |
| Zhang, Baker, Farmer 2023 | Online computing course | High vs low dose of time-management prompts | No significant effect | [17] |
| Patterson 2018 | Online course (a statistics MOOC, per a search summary) | Commitment device, alert tool, distraction blocker | Commitment: +24% time, +0.29 SD grade, 40% more likely to finish (11 points, search summary); alert and blocker: no difference | [22] |
| Yeomans and Reich 2017 | 3 HarvardX courses | Open-ended plan prompt at course start | Completion +29%; certificate payment +40% | [23] |
| Felkey et al. 2021 | Nearly 1,000 students | Text microcommitments plus social feed vs simple nudges | Exam score about 3 percentage points higher, driven by online and hybrid classes | [25] |
| Damgaard and Nielsen 2018 | Education nudge review | Nudges often work; biggest where the barrier bites; can backfire by crowding out motivation or pressuring | Not on page | [24] |

- Pattern: scheduling help and reminders alone mostly give nothing on grades; a costly commitment and a start-of-course plan sometimes do [19][20][22][23].
  o Build the engine as plan plus commitment plus visible pace, not as a notification stream.
- Replication of planning prompts was mixed (four trials, one course gained 19%): unverified, seen in a search summary.
- Course Signals retention claims were challenged as reverse causation; treat early-warning results as contested [26].
- A text-nudge study on summer enrolment (Castleman and Page) is the classic positive case; not opened (unverified).
- One-hour pitch test measures novelty, not learning; the null results above are the honest base rate for grades.

## 5. The "ahead" moment: progress feedback, self-efficacy and the risks

| Mechanism | Evidence | Number or finding | Ref |
|---|---|---|---|
| Progress monitoring | Harkin et al. 2016, 138 randomised trials, 19,951 people | Monitoring d = 1.98; goal attainment d = 0.40; larger when progress recorded or reported | [9] |
| Subgoal success raises efficacy | Complex-task studies, summarised | Reaching a subgoal raised self-efficacy, satisfaction, persistence | [8] |
| Small wins | Diary study, workers | Progress the most prominent event on best days | [13] |
| Licensing | Fishbach and Dhar 2005 | A sense of progress frees people to pursue conflicting goals; planning future progress did too | [27] |
| Complacency | Amir and Ariely 2008 | Markers help when distance is unclear; when clear they can breed complacency and cut performance | [28] |
| Where to look | Koo and Fishbach 2012 | Early: focus on what is done; near the end: focus on what remains | [29] |
| Tool abandonment | Retention benchmarks, vendor compilation | Day 30: productivity about 4%, education about 2 to 3% | [33] |
| Over-planning | Practitioner posts only | No peer-reviewed study found (unverified) | none |

- No study found of students who saw themselves ahead of schedule and then changed behaviour; the ahead moment is untested.
  o The pilot must measure effort in the week after the student gets ahead, not only grades.
- Time management links more to wellbeing than to grades, so claim calm and control as well as marks [18].
- Harkin's trials were mostly health goals (unverified, from a search summary); say "goals", not "study".
- Participation was high in a free university programme yet grades did not move [19]; take-up is not impact.

## 6. Five design rules for the engine

| Rule | Design rule | Evidence | Ref |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Goal size | Each goal is specific, finishable in one sitting and still a stretch; show a few, not dozens | Proximal subgoals built mastery; specific difficult goals beat vague; too many subgoals cut satisfaction; segmenting counters the planning fallacy | [7][8][6] |
| 2 When to show progress | Log every finished goal; show distance done early in a task and distance left near the end; add markers when the finish line is unclear | Monitoring d = 0.40; small-area effect; markers help under uncertainty | [9][29][28] |
| 3 Framing ahead | Show the lead as a buffer to spend on a hard week, not as permission to stop; pilot it against coasting | Progress can license conflicting choices; clear markers can breed complacency | [27][28] |
| 4 A miss | Re-spread the remaining goals from the next fresh start, with no streak reset and no shaming copy | Self-forgiveness predicted less later procrastination (134 first-years, correlational); fresh starts rise at weeks, months, semesters | [30][31] |
| 5 Social | One named buddy or one visible commitment on the same goal; no public rankings by default | Public reporting gave larger attainment gains; commitment plus social feed gave about 3 exam points; nudges can pressure | [9][25][24] |

- Rules 3 and 5 rest on indirect evidence: licensing is from consumer lab studies and the exam gain came with a ranked feed [27][25].
- For leaderboards, streak resets and relatedness size, see gamification-research.md.

## 7. Three numbers for the 3-minute pitch

- "37 students predicted 33.9 days for their thesis and took 55.5; about 30% finished on time." [6]
- "Across 138 randomised trials with 19,951 people, monitoring goal progress raised goal attainment, d = 0.40." [9]
- "In one online course, a commitment device made students 40% more likely to finish and lifted grades 0.29 SD; alerts alone did nothing." [22]
- Say it honestly: one psychology cohort, general goal trials and one online course; reminders alone gave no grade gain for 9,000+ students [19], so we test, not claim.

## 8. Pros and cons of the small-goals engine, as the evidence shows them

| Pros | Evidence | Cons | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Students under-estimate their own work, so splitting corrects a real bias | [6] | Scheduling help and reminders gave precise nulls on grades at scale | [19][20][21] |
| Proximal subgoals built mastery and efficacy where distal goals did not | [7][8] | Starting early alone did not improve grades | [16] |
| Monitoring progress raises attainment, d = 0.40 | [9] | Progress can license coasting; markers can breed complacency | [27][28] |
| A costly commitment beat alerts: +40% completion | [22] | Splitters exist (Ahead, UMN calculator, a student GPT) | [42][44][43] |
| Start-of-course plan prompts: completion +29% | [23] | Deadline-precommitment flagship is retracted | [10][11] |
| Plans free attention from unfinished goals | [15] | Unfinished-task memory effect not robust | [14] |
| No product opened pairs unit outline, cohort pacing and ahead line | [42][43][44] | App retention is low: day 30 about 2 to 4% | [33] |
| Small, specific goals suit the weekly when-and-where plans already in the pack | see gamification-research.md | Time management helps wellbeing more than marks | [18] |
| Social commitment plus nudges gave about 3 exam points | [25] | The ahead moment has no direct study; coordinator approval untested | none |

- Verdict: sound mechanisms, thin and mixed effect on grades at scale; pitch confidence, calm and starting, then measure.
