## 5. Pros and cons, one block per live candidate
- M2, practice studio for pre-service primary teachers:
  o Pro: a real, documented gap; future teachers get about 8 hours of music training and must teach it anyway.
  o Pro: the phone listens and watches, never plays; sidesteps the AI-in-music backlash.
  o Pro: cheap, demoable, testable with peers and guitars in one session.
  o Con: narrow user; judges may ask why not Yousician for teachers.
  o Con: camera coaching is research-grade; the demo must stay on microphone pitch and chords.
  o Con: needs a sponsor-university unit to anchor the pilot; not yet confirmed.
- Q, small goals (Carlos's revision of Questline):
  o Pro: aims at procrastination, the problem most students name; weekly when-and-where plans carry d = .65.
  o Pro: progress visible and near; goal-gradient and progress effects are well evidenced.
  o Pro: no game skin to wear off; avoids the narrative cost and the week-4 novelty dip.
  o Pro: any unit, any discipline; the coordinator's content and assessments stay as they are.
  o Con: AI task splitters and planners exist; the unit mapping, cohort pacing and "ahead" line must be the difference.
  o Con: a planner can look small on stage; the "two weeks ahead" moment has to be the demo's peak.
  o Keep light: one study buddy on the same goal and the class task bar, not raids; relatedness is still the largest lever.
  o Evidence: procrastination-small-goals-evidence.md. Monitoring progress d = .40 across 138 trials; reminders alone gave no grade gain at scale, so we test, not claim.
  o Risk: being ahead can license coasting; show the lead as a buffer for a hard week, never as permission to stop.
- Q+M2, one engine shown on one unit:
  o Pro: general answer plus specific demo, which is how both past winners pitched.
  o Pro: the music energy stays; the engine makes it more than a music app.
  o Pro: instrument practice is the purest small-goal loop: one chord, one switch, one song, daily.
  o Pro: learning by teaching has evidence in university music theory, so an Apprentice-style quest fits inside.
  o Con: scope; the demo must be one path, not two products. Cut until it fits 60 seconds.
  o Con: coordinator approval of a generated map is untested; keep it to one click in the demo.
- B, the Apprentice:
  o Pro: clearest "not a tutor" and "why not Claude" answers of any candidate.
  o Con: not new; evidence from samples of 20 to 41; weaker stage experience.
- Carlos's devil's-advocate lines on M, for the meeting:
  o "We both framed this from our own story. Yousician already has 20 million monthly users and sells an AI guitar teacher."
  o "As proposed it fails 'not a tutor', 'mapped to outcomes' and 'flips the metric'. Past winners cleared all three."
  o "Cultural preservation needs First Nations consent we cannot get by Wednesday."
  o "The gap that survives is the user: a future primary teacher who has to teach music after eight hours of training."
  o "Keep the guitar on stage. Change who is holding it and why."
