## John's feedback
- Learning outcomes are the North Star. Assessment exists to test them; GenAI made the old tests easy to paste.
  o The real question: how do you test learning outcomes in the GenAI age?
  o Prompting as evidence of understanding is one valid method. Validate it with someone who holds a teaching degree.
- Do not say "API" or "key" to a general audience. Students will not paste subscription keys.
  o Manage the model on the platform; abstract it for the institution. Make it seamless.
- Consumer subscriptions (ChatGPT, Claude) have terms that forbid wrapper or extension use. BYO subscription can kill the product.
  o Managed endpoints and API access are infrastructure and fine.
- Institutions already license AI: Flinders gives every student Gemini Enterprise; UNSW gives ChatGPT (both unverified, as heard).
- Consider discipline-specific niches, for example medicine case studies with interactive AI patients, not only a general campus tool.
  o Niche disciplines may need more than one platform to reach their outcomes.
- Presentation: bring the idea to life for an audience that knows nothing about it.

- Present: Simone, Justin, Carlos; the mentor joined; Stanley rotated in; Jeff for a few minutes; main-room wrap-up.

### Simone's new idea: lower the barrier to music education
- Reflection: which subject never got AI support? Music. Maths and English already have tutors.
- Gap claimed: online AI-assisted music learning is shallow and covers few instruments.
- Shape: a digitised instrument on the phone, piano through to a Chinese flute; AI personalises the lesson to the student's level.
- Users: students without funds, without a school music programme, with a disability, or far from teachers.
- The AI never generates music for the student; it is a vehicle. Student keeps autonomy (team rule from Monday).
- Extra framing: cultural preservation of rare instruments; access for low-SES and disabled students.
- Core screen idea: a digital guitar with strings you poke; visual aids for real-instrument owners later.
- Her answer to "why not ChatGPT": it cannot teach you to play; a chat user would have to build their own tool.
- Simone: "it doesn't have to be music, but it could be the most creative thing we do."

### Justin's add: camera coaching for guitar
- Phone camera tracks fingers, recognises chords and hand position, suggests corrections.
- Guitar-specific; piano hides the keys under the palm.

### Mentor's feedback
- Arts and music push back hardest on AI; adoptability is the risk; many musicians would refuse an AI teacher.
- Ideas so far lack sensitivity: "research is also about researching emotion, not just statistics."
- Not a reason to drop it: "keep that idea, experiment", but expect "how can AI know music when it can't hear".
- Music datasets are hard to get if the team wants to train anything.
- Manpower: three people; do not pick an idea so big you choke on the day. Practical and demonstrable.
- Proof of concept can be faked on stage. 2025: front end only, no back end; the QR scan did not work.

### Stanley's feedback
- The stigma is about buying AI-generated music, not about AI teaching the basics of an instrument.
- If music, decide now and narrow: music theory, or string instruments, not "music" in general.

### Jeff's feedback in the room

- Music is where personal learning and social learning overlap and interact.
