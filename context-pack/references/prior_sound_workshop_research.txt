 01 / THE RECOMMENDATION



 A place to make noise.
 A playful, welcoming music app with a clear identity: a colourful sound
 workshop built for curiosity, confidence and making things.

 Use expressive, slightly irregular headings; readable everyday text; a warm canvas; a decisive cobalt
 action colour; and a small family of shapes that visibly respond to sound.

 The direction I would choose
   Element                      Recommended starting point


   Personality                  Childlike curiosity, generous space, confident colour and simple humour.

   Typography                   Grandstander 650 for short display headings. Atkinson Hyperlegible Next 400/650 for
                                reading and controls.

   Palette                      Cream + dark plum ink + cobalt, with coral and yellow as expressive accents.

   Signature                    A repeatable family of sound stamps: circles, arches, bars and loops.

   Behaviour                    Make a sound quickly, explore freely, replay, undo and hear what changed.




                   Try                                             Hear                                 Make

 Why it can last
 Simple shapes, a consistent drawing method and a recognisable voice are easy to extend. Girard's
 long-lived figure family is a useful visual precedent. Yoto and Nickelodeon demonstrate how an
 organising motif can connect many different touchpoints.

 Avoid treating fake grain as proof of humanity. A clean digital design can have character; an artificial
 paper texture can be just as formulaic as a gradient.

 Test actual words, not just an alphabet
 Check "Play", "Listen", "Loop", "Record", "Stop" and "Save", plus I/l/1, O/0, rn/m and b/d/p/q. Compare
 at the same visible size; nominal font size alone can be misleading.

 Keep live text, preserve useful spacing when users enlarge it, and let labels wrap. Make room for
 longer translated text. Typography for Arabic, Indic and other scripts needs a compatible family and
 review by fluent readers.

 Sizes above are web CSS starting points. Native iOS points and Android dp require platform-specific decisions. A preference
 setting is useful only if the enlarged interface still works.

 A practical allocation
 Start with roughly 60-70% quiet canvas, 20-30% structured content and 10% strong accents on a
 typical navigation screen. This is a compositional heuristic. A playing surface may use more colour
 when it helps separate playable objects.

 No research establishes that this palette makes children more creative. Choose it for identity and visual hierarchy, then test
 comfort and recognition with the intended audience.

 Apply colour accessibly
 Normal text needs at least 4.5:1; large text has a 3:1 exception. Relevant controls and graphics also
 need sufficient contrast. Never use colour alone for a musical category, a selection or a result: add a
 label, symbol, position or outline.

 Check focus, pressed, selected, disabled and error states separately. Test real screen brightness and
 lighting. An attractive pastel surface does not automatically provide a visible boundary against cream.

 Make the comparison fair
 The three boards use the same headline and comparable controls. Change visual direction while
 holding task, words and interaction constant. Ask who each version seems to be for, then observe
 whether people can actually use it.

 product promise, then resolve the concrete usability failures.

 13 / SHAPE, ILLUSTRATION AND MOTION



 Give the world a visual grammar
 A small family of sound stamps
 Build from circles, rounded rectangles, arches, bars and loops. Repeat one line weight, similar corner
 behaviour and a limited palette. Slight irregularity can live in large artwork; playable hit areas remain
 stable and generous.

 Let the shapes behave: a touched pad compresses briefly, a held note has a steady visible state, and
 a repeating phrase has a clear playhead. These are interface feedback ideas, not universal mappings
 between shape, colour and musical meaning.

   Layer                           Direction


   Illustration                    Flat, confident colour; one coherent drawing method. Choose a few useful scenes rather
                                   than unrelated decorative objects.

   Characters                      Optional companions with specific roles. Expressions can be funny or curious; keep the
                                   learner's work at the centre.

   Material                        Crisp paper-like surfaces; texture only in nonessential art. Keep labels and controls
                                   clean.

   Motion                          Brief response to an action. Provide static equivalents, reduced motion and a way to turn
                                   down effects.

   Sound                           Separate instructional speech, instrument audio and decorative effects. Let users control
                                   them.

   Cultural instruments            Use accurate names, credited collaborators and context approved by the relevant
                                   community.

 14 / INTERACTION AND TONE



 Make freedom something you can
 do
 The welcoming feeling should come from the experience: learners can
 start, hear a result, change their mind and keep something they made.

   Moment                       Recommended behaviour / wording


   Start                        One obvious invitation: "Make a sound." Offer an optional guided start alongside free
                                exploration.

   Try                          Tap as a complete action. Give an alternative to every essential drag interaction.

   Hear                         Make the relationship between the touched object and its sound clear, with a visible state
                                too.

   Learn                        Show a useful prompt: "Try leaving a space between these beats."

   Recover                      Undo, clear and restart stay visible. Explain an error in plain language.

   Keep                         "Save my sound" or "Listen again" gives ownership of the result.

   Return                       "Ready to try another sound?" keeps the invitation open. Avoid guilt and lost-progress
                                pressure.


 Feedback should carry information
 "You made the second note longer" tells a learner what happened. "You are a genius!" gives much
 less guidance. Celebrate effort and exploration, while distinguishing free creation from an exercise
 with a specific musical goal.

 A decorative click should not mask timing or pitch. Offer visual timing cues and optional haptics
 where supported, while being clear that some auditory learning objectives need adapted teaching or
 alternatives.

 18 / IMPLEMENTATION DIRECTION



 A brief you can hand to a designer
 Design a music-learning app as a colourful sound workshop. The feeling is playful, welcoming, curious
 and free to experiment. Use a consistent visual world that can welcome both children and older
 beginners.

 Use Grandstander 650 for short headings and Atkinson Hyperlegible Next 400/650 for reading and
 controls. Begin with 18/27 px body text and visible 18 px action labels; support enlargement and
 comfortable spacing.

 Use cream #FFF8E8 as the main canvas; plum #2B2341 for text; cobalt #3658D4 for the main action
 with white text. Use coral #FF7954, yellow #FFD65C, mint #A7DFC3 and lilac #C4B4E8 for sound
 objects and illustration with plum labels.

 Create a small family of printed geometric sound stamps: circles, arches, bars and loops. Keep
 drawing, corners and line weights related. Let expressive art be a little uneven while controls stay
 visually stable.

 Prioritise a playable instrument and a clear first action. Provide generous targets: start at 48-56 CSS
 px for web controls and larger playable pads where possible. Use labels plus icons and offer
 keyboard and tap alternatives to essential dragging.

 Make input produce purposeful sound and visible feedback. Offer reduced motion and separate
 decorative audio. Let users explore, undo, replay and save their work. Use specific, encouraging
 prompts.

 Keep gradients, glossy 3D and decorative textures out of this starting direction. Keep reading
 surfaces clear. Build the home, playing state, prompt, settings and error state in the same system
 before adding a mascot or more artwork.

 Validate with the intended learners, including disabled learners, rather than describing the result as accessible on
 appearance alone. The exact fonts, ratios and target sizes above are implementation starting points to test.
