# Carmen innovation and competitor audit
Version 2.0 | Research checked 9 November 2026

## Verdict
The individual proposed innovations are already represented in products or research.
The evidence does not justify calling Carmen the first AI musical partner, first gesture instrument,
first adaptive music tutor, first AI teaching-example generator or first accessible digital music suite.

There is no verified exclusive USP yet. This is a substantive correction to the earlier ensemble-led pitch,
not a reason to abandon useful functionality. A USP can be a buyer-specific, demonstrated advantage;
it need not be a globally unprecedented component or a patentable invention.

The strongest candidate to test is a primary music-learning environment in which the student:
creates a phrase; directs a musical response; inspects a verified declared change; predicts/listens/revises;
and demonstrates a related skill independently, while the teacher can inspect assistance and evidence.

No reviewed source established that complete proposed workflow as an exclusive gap.
Public documentation cannot prove absence; an existing product or a teacher using several tools may reproduce it.

## Method and limits
The update used 31 targeted web queries plus direct retrieval of official product/help pages,
primary research and technical/model documentation. The search log records the actual queries.
The comparison matrix has 29 entries spanning products, programmes, research precedents and model tooling.
These are not 29 directly interchangeable commercial competitors or 29 independent educational trials.

Public documentation was reviewed, not paid products through authenticated accounts.
Product statements establish documented claims, not observed reliability or learning effectiveness.
Publication/availability distinctions are retained: Maru is private early access, Violy's cited announcement
is market-limited, and research installations are not treated as established school services.
A QuaverMusic URL redirected to a Health/PE page; that page was excluded from music claims.
Yousician's direct page returned 403; official indexed content was available.
MusiQuest's product page returned no readable text; official indexed content and its curriculum PDF were used.

## Closest comparisons
Moises' school offer documents learner-conditioned generated instrumental parts.
Its Studio also documents editable output and natural-language project actions.
Do not differentiate Carmen merely by saying the learner retains control or the AI acts as a bandmate. [U01-U02]

Soundverse explicitly describes short audio teaching examples, quizzes and guided learning formats.
A pedagogical prompt plus generated audio is consequently insufficient as the innovation argument. [U07]

EarMaster describes adaptive questions, sing/clap-back and alternative visual inputs.
A difficulty slider, changing exercise length or call-and-response is not novel by itself. [U08]

MusiQuest is particularly close on composition-led learning and connected musical representations.
Charanga and QuaverMusic establish a high bar for a usable primary curriculum and teacher support. [U26; U30; U39]

AUMI and Clarion show that access involves configuration and teaching, not merely sound production.
Gesture interaction must be compared for actual access benefit and fatigue. [U10; U40]

## Proposed technical application
Use learned response planning inside a musically constrained interaction:
1. Preserve the student's original event phrase and stated intention.
2. Use the selected learning objective, phrase features, attempt facts and learner choices as context.
3. A local trained model proposes a bounded response plan, or a reviewed musical model proposes notes.
4. A symbolic compiler/checker verifies exactly the properties claimed for that task.
5. The learner hears, predicts, redirects, compares and revises the response.
6. A fresh independent task checks application with teaching cues removed and access retained.
7. Teacher evidence distinguishes input, model action, assistance, decisions and independent performance.

The model is not the music engine, a mind reader or the assessor of creative quality.
A verified symbolic change is not proof that only one perceptual experience changed:
changing dynamics can also alter acoustic timbre, and adding a part can change harmony and texture.
State the exact invariants verified and keep musical/educational judgement with humans.

## Candidate advantage, stated narrowly
Carmen is proposed to make it practical to teach a mixed-ability primary group through student-owned
musical experiments, using real local AI planning with checked musical changes and inspectable learning evidence.

This is a positioning and implementation hypothesis. It is not a claim that every competitor lacks these properties.
The combination must outperform a reasonable existing workflow on an outcome that a purchaser values.

Potential technical assets are the task/constraint representation, response-policy data reviewed by educators,
reliable input/audio/notation integration and evidence of good teaching decisions.
They are not a generic model wrapper, an AI label or ownership of children's behavioural data.

## Demonstrations needed before claiming an edge
- A teacher selects a goal; the response depends materially on the actual learner phrase.
- Two different observed difficulties lead to different, justified checks or responses.
- A model-generated plan that violates a constraint is rejected before playback.
- A learner can reject or change the partner's contribution without losing original work.
- A fresh task supports an appropriately limited claim of independent understanding.
- All primary bands have meaningfully different starter experiences, with an older extension.
- At least one target school device runs the actual model within a declared budget.
- A teacher can achieve a useful class outcome with manageable preparation/support.

These demonstrate functionality and feasibility, not comparative effectiveness or uniqueness.

## Comparison programme
Use a practical Musicca/EarMaster/teacher workflow for foundational skill comparisons,
and a suitably licensed Moises/MusicFirst or other creation workflow for creative-task comparisons.
Use adult/educator trials first where child eligibility or account terms are unresolved.
Do not force a weak competitor baseline or let Carmen receive more teaching time.

Compare:
A. Fixed instrument and reviewed feedback.
B. Adjustable instrument and reviewed response rules.
C. The same environment with learned response planning.
D. Gesture input versus equivalent accessible touch/keyboard input, only where useful.

Separate usability, immediate performance, delayed independent application, musical agency,
teacher workload, delivery cost and willingness to pay. A small feasibility pilot is not a definitive trial.

## Decision rules
Keep the candidate claim conditional until users can choose Carmen for an evidenced benefit.
If model planning is no better than good rules, report that and revise the technology claim.
If gesture input worsens access or audio responsiveness, keep it experimental/off.
If another product reproduces the workflow more simply or cheaply, change the proposition or consider integration.
Do not fabricate a gap to justify an already chosen idea.

## What remains unresolved
Authenticated competitor trials; a market-specific purchase pathway; model selection/weights/rights;
device profiling; educator review across F-6; cultural partnerships; comparative and delayed learning evidence;
the economics of content/support; and any separate patent/freedom-to-operate analysis.
